The bar gap

France's 49,385 bars aren't evenly distributed. Corsica has 21 per 10,000 residents. The suburbs of Paris have 3.

2026-06-02 4 min read

France has 49,385 active débits de boissons — bars, cafés, brasseries — and roughly 65 million metropolitan residents. The national average works out to 7.45 bars per 10,000 people. But a national average is a liar. Zoom into any one département and that figure can be anywhere from 3 to 21. Where you live determines whether France's legendary café culture is actually yours.

Corsica is France's bar capital

Haute-Corse (2B) tops the ranking at 21.1 bars per 10,000 residents — nearly three times the national average. Corse-du-Sud (2A) follows at 16.9. Together, the two Corsican départements rank 1st and 3rd out of 96. Between them, the island has 666 active bars for a permanent population of under 350,000.

The runner-up is Lozère (48) at 17.6 — France's least populous département, 76,000 residents, 135 bars. Then Cantal (15) at 16.6, Hautes-Alpes (05) at 15.6. The top of the ranking is a list of France's remotest, most mountainous, most tourism-dependent territories. The bar isn't just a leisure amenity here — it's often the only meeting place in the village.

6.9× the gap between Haute-Corse (21.1 per 10K) and Essonne (3.1 per 10K) — the highest and lowest départements in metropolitan France

Paris punches way above its weight

The surprise is Paris. Département 75 — the city itself, excluding its suburbs — ranks 7th in France at 14.6 bars per 10,000 residents. That puts Paris ahead of Bretagne, ahead of the Alps, ahead of the Mediterranean coast. For a city of 2.1 million, it's a remarkable density.

The explanation is structural: Paris was built before the car. Its tight 19th-century street grid, where apartments sit above shops and every block has a corner café, makes bar viability a function of foot traffic rather than car parking. Add two million daily commuters and several million tourists, and the 3,121 registered bars in the city's 75km² make sense as economic geography, not just culture.

The suburb that doesn't drink — at least not in bars

Cross the Périphérique, and something shifts. The six départements that ring Paris occupy the bottom third of the national ranking. Essonne (91): 3.1. Yvelines (78) and Hauts-de-Seine (92): 3.3. Val-d'Oise (95): 3.7. Seine-et-Marne (77): 3.8. Val-de-Marne (94): 4.0.

Paris (14.6) has 4.7 times as many bars per head as its nearest suburban département. You can board the RER at Châtelet and ride 40 minutes to a town with no bar within walking distance — no café to open a laptop, no counter to lean on before work, no terrace in July. The suburb wasn't built for that. It was built for the car and the supermarket.

Same ring, two kinds of absence

Readers of the medical deserts story will recognise the pattern. The APL score — France's measure of GP access — is low in almost the same ring. Seine-et-Marne (77) has an average APL of 2.11 across its communes, below the official desert threshold of 2.5. Yvelines (78): 2.34. Val-d'Oise (95): 2.31. Essonne (91): 2.46.

The correspondence isn't perfect: Hauts-de-Seine (92), the wealthy western suburb, has 3.3 bars per 10K but a reasonable APL of 3.09 — it lacks bars without being a medical desert. But the broad pattern holds. The postwar commuter belt, built fast and cheap along motorway corridors, never developed the commercial street life that makes both a GP practice and a neighbourhood café economically viable. High rents, low footfall, and a population that drives everywhere keep both the doctor and the barman out.

The village café has been closing across rural France for decades — but in the places it survives, it survives hard. Corsica, the Massif Central, the Alps: these are the territories where the bar remains an institution, not an amenity. The new bar deserts aren't the empty countryside. They're the full suburbs.

A note on the data. Bar counts come from the same SIRENE dataset as Story 4: active establishments with NAF code 56.30Z (débits de boissons), March 2026 snapshot, geocoded by INSEE. Population figures are the INSEE 2021 commune-level totals included in the DREES APL 2023 file, summed by département. The analysis covers 96 metropolitan départements; overseas territories (971–976) are excluded due to their unique geography and the way département codes were assigned in the source data.
