The bar capital of France is at 1,650 metres

Which commune has the most bars per resident? Not Paris, not Bretagne. The answer is a ski resort in the Alps — and so are the next seven.

2026-05-17 4 min read

France has 49,385 registered bars. We already know where they cluster by département: the Norte, Bretagne, the Mediterranean coast. But which commune has the most, per resident? The question is simple. The answer is surprising.

Les Deux Alpes, in Isère, has 39 registered bars for a permanent population of 1,933. That's 201.8 bars per 10,000 residents. For comparison, Paris's most bar-dense arrondissement — the 11th, home of Oberkampf and République — manages 27.1. The ski resort beats the bar neighbourhood by a factor of seven.

Every bar capital is a tourist destination

Bars per 10,000 residents — communes with 500+ permanent residents

Eight of the top ten communes by this measure are Alpine ski stations: Les Deux Alpes, Morzine, Orcières, Risoul, Châtel, Val-d'Isère, Les Gets, Courchevel, Tignes. The logic is straightforward — these communes count 2,000 permanent residents, but welcome 30,000 visitors on a busy February weekend. The bars were built for the season. The licences stay on the books year-round. The population denominator never swells to match.

The non-alpine entries in the top 20 are equally tourist-shaped. Rogliano is the northernmost cape of Corsica — a stunning headland village that fills with summer visitors. La Roche-Bernard and Île-aux-Moines are Morbihan sailing country. Saint-Tropez (34 bars, 3,578 residents, 95/10k) needs no introduction.

Among major cities, Paris 11e leads — and Rouen surprises

Bars per 10,000 residents — communes or arrondissements with 100,000+ permanent residents

Strip away the seasonal distortion and focus on communes with at least 100,000 permanent residents. Paris's 11th arrondissement wins at 27.1 per 10,000 — a neighbourhood synonymous with nightlife, dense with brasseries, bistrots, and late bars around the Oberkampf metro. But the second entry is a genuine surprise: Rouen, at 20.8. Metz, at 15.7, edges out Paris's own 18th. Saint-Étienne — not a city usually associated with drinking culture — places eighth at 15.0.

Toulouse (6.4/10k) and Montpellier (8.5/10k), both with large student populations, rank lower than their reputation suggests. The university effect and the bar density effect don't always align — part of the student economy operates outside the SIRENE formal licence system.

Two-thirds of France has no bar at all

The other end of the table is where the story gets serious. 23,266 communes — 66.4% of all French communes — have no registered bar. That covers 11.3 million people, roughly one in six French residents. These aren't abstract statistics: they trace the same diagonale du vide that shows up in the medical deserts map and the national bar density map. Rural France is emptying of services, and the village café is no exception.

The bar is not just a cultural institution. It's one of the last remaining gathering places in many small communes — the alternative to the closed post office, the shuttered bakery, the merged school. When it closes, nothing replaces it. And 23,266 communes are already at zero.

A note on the data. Bar locations come from the Where France drinks story dataset: 49,385 active establishments with NAF code 56.30Z (débits de boissons), geocoded from INSEE SIRENE + SIRENE Géolocalisation, March 2026 snapshot. Population figures are the commune-level resident population from the DREES APL dataset (INSEE base 2021), which covers all 35,014 French communes. Per-capita rates use permanent resident population, which understates the true density in tourist communes.
