The bar at altitude

Ask which French commune has the most bars per capita, and the answer isn't a Parisian arrondissement — it's a mountain village in the Pyrenees.

2026-05-09 4 min read

France has 49,385 registered débits de boissons — bars, cafés, brasseries, and pubs. Averaged across roughly 35,000 communes and 67 million residents, that works out to 0.73 bars per thousand people. Most major cities sit somewhere between one and two. Then there's Gavarnie-Gèdre.

The mountain station pattern

Gavarnie-Gèdre is a commune in the Hautes-Pyrénées — the approach to the Cirque de Gavarnie, a UNESCO-listed glacial amphitheatre that draws several hundred thousand visitors each year. Its permanent registered population: 341. Its bars: seven.

28× Gavarnie-Gèdre's bar density above the French national average

The rest of the top ten reads like a ski resort directory: Les Deux Alpes (20.2/1k), Chamrousse (19.3), Morzine (19.2), Puy-Saint-Vincent (17.9), Montgenèvre (17.5), Orcières (16.1), Risoul (15.2). Nine of the top fifteen communes are Alpine or Pyrenean mountain stations, most of them household names for French skiers.

Counted against the wrong population

The explanation lies in the gap between official population and actual occupancy. Morzine, fourth on the list with 51 bars and 2,660 permanent residents (19.2/1k), regularly hosts upward of 30,000 people during peak ski season. Val-d'Isère (1,580 registered residents) sees 45,000 skiers in February. Courchevel's 2,311 residents share infrastructure built for a luxury resort that hosts a hundred times that number each season.

The bars, restaurants, and hotels in these communes were licensed to serve that transient population. INSEE's SIRENE business registry records each establishment against its commune's permanent address — so the per-capita ratio is computed against a denominator that understates actual clientele by a factor of ten or more. The mountain bar density isn't a cultural phenomenon; it's a statistical one.

Islands and villages

Not every top-ranked commune is a ski resort. Île-d'Houat, fifth on the list at 18.5 per thousand, is a Breton island in the Golfe du Morbihan with 216 permanent residents and four bars. The same mismatch logic applies: in summer, the island's ferry brings day-trippers and campers by the boatload. The bars stay registered year-round; the visitors don't. La Roche-Bernard (11.5/1k), a medieval river town on the Vilaine, makes the top 20 through similar seasonal dynamics — more weekend visitors than permanent residents, and the café infrastructure to match.

The strangest outlier is Serverette in Lozère, ninth on the list at 16.1 per thousand. This is not a resort. It's a pastoral commune of 248 people in the Margeride plateau — no ski lift, no tourist trail. Four bars. In a village this small, a few cafés that simply haven't closed can dominate the ratio entirely. It's a different kind of density: not seasonal, but structural. The village bar as social institution, counted mathematically as excess.

Lille leads France's major cities at 1.88 bars per thousand — more than twice the national average, reflecting the dense café culture of the north. Paris, spread across twenty arrondissements, averages around 1.5. The first arrondissement (Paris 1er) reaches 7.73/1k — built more for its half-million daily workers and tourists than its 16,000 registered residents. Even so, that's less than half the density of Gavarnie-Gèdre.

The bar-per-capita ranking isn't really a measure of where the French drink most. It's a measure of where bars were licensed relative to the people who officially call that place home. The answer: in the mountains. Built for skiers who arrive in January, counted against permanent populations that stay all year.

A note on the data. Bar counts come from INSEE SIRENE (NAF 56.30Z, active establishments, March 2026), geocoded by INSEE's statistical geolocation file. Each bar is assigned to a commune via the commune code in its SIRENE record. Population denominators are from the DREES APL 2023 dataset (INSEE 2021 population base) — the same source used in the medical deserts story. Analysis covers 11,201 communes with population ≥ 200 and at least one matched bar record. Paris is modelled as twenty arrondissements, each with its own bar count and population.
