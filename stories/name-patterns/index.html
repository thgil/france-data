The name game

France has 35,014 communes. One of them is called Y. Here's what the full list reveals.

2026-05-22 4 min read

France has more communes than Germany, Italy, and Spain combined — 35,014 of them, each with an official INSEE code, a budget, and a name. Those names accumulate over a millennium: Gallo-Roman roots, Frankish suffixes, monastic dedications, river crossings, feudal holdings, and the occasional administrative accident. Run them all through a computer and patterns emerge.

Most names are 7–9 characters long

The average French commune name is 11.8 characters — though that average is pulled rightward by a long tail of hyphenated place-names, many created when villages merged in the 1970s. The modal names cluster between seven and nine characters: Beaumont, Verrières, Le Mesnil. Short, specific, memorable.

Y

Y
Somme (80) · population 89 · INSEE code 80829

France's shortest commune name is not an abbreviation, a typo, or a joke. Y is a village in the Somme, named from a Celtic root meaning yew tree. Population 89. It has a church (twelfth-century), a post office, and a primary school. The name predates the French state by a thousand years. It is also entirely unsearchable on the internet, which the residents may or may not find amusing.

One in nine communes is a saint

The single most common word in French commune names is saint — appearing 4,443 times across all names, as a first word, a middle element, or a qualifier. 3,898 communes start with Saint or Sainte: that's 11.1% of the total. The country was named by people who believed in patron saints, and the infrastructure reflects it.

The most popular saint is Martin — 255 commune names contain his name. Then Jean (170 mentions), Pierre (180), and Germain (120). Saint-Martin-de-… appears everywhere from Bretagne to Provence: France has 9 communes named simply Saint-Marcel, and variants of Martin cover the map in every direction.

Sainte-Colombe: France's most repeated commune name

Across all 35,014 communes, Sainte-Colombe appears 12 times — the most of any single name. These twelve villages have nothing in common except their dedication to a third-century martyr: they are scattered across twelve different départements, from Hautes-Alpes to Yonne, from Charente-Maritime to Rhône.

Shared names are more common than you might expect: 3,700 communes — one in nine — share their name with at least one other commune somewhere in France. That's how the French postal code exists: the INSEE code was invented precisely because names don't uniquely identify places.

At the other end of the spectrum

The longest commune name in France is 45 characters long and contains two saints:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson
45 characters · Marne (51)

It was created in 1972 when three villages — Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont, Saint-Genest, and Isson — merged into a single commune. The runner-up, at 43 characters, is Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur, which joined four villages in Haute-Saône.

Long names cluster in the 1970s: France's loi Marcellin of 1971 encouraged rural communes to merge, and many of them preserved all their old names by hyphenating. The policy was mostly abandoned when it became clear that no one wanted to live in a commune they couldn't pronounce. About half the post-1971 mergers have since been reversed.

The average name is 11.8 characters, the mode is 7–9, the minimum is 1. Y sits at the far left of the distribution: one letter, no hyphens, 89 residents, and a name older than France itself. It's the exception that makes the pattern visible.

A note on the data. Commune names and populations come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset joined to administrative boundaries — the same source file used in Story 2 (medical deserts). The dataset covers 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Population figures are from the INSEE 2021 census base.
