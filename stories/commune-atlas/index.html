What France calls its communes

35,014 names — one letter long or 45, saint-heavy, and duplicated across a dozen departments in ways no postal worker would envy.

2026-05-21 5 min read

The shortest commune name in France is a single letter: Y. It sits in the Somme (département 80), population 89, and it has been there since at least the thirteenth century. Its postal code is 80829. Its name on a French administrative database looks like a typo.

Below Y in the alphabet of French communes, everything gets longer. A lot longer. The median commune name runs to ten characters; the average, 11.8. Thirteen communes manage just two. One reaches 45. The distribution is a small story all on its own.

The saint economy

Of 35,014 communes, 3,882 open with Saint- or Sainte-. That's one in nine. If the saints seceded tomorrow, they'd form a country of 3,882 municipalities and — given France's fragmented territorial geography — they'd still be the 16th-largest country in Europe by commune count. Saint dominates (3,587 communes); Sainte adds 295 more.

After the saints, La Chapelle is the most common prefix with 152 communes — every one of them presumably sitting around a small chapel that someone built a millennium ago and the village never forgot. Villers comes next (105), then Villeneuve (70), then Pont (62), then Fontaine (58). French commune naming is a religion-and-geography project.

Twelve Sainte-Colombes

France has 35,014 communes but only 32,764 unique names. The other 2,250 names are shared — sometimes by two or three communes, occasionally by more. Sainte-Colombe holds the record: twelve communes in twelve different departments all claim the name. Write a letter to "Sainte-Colombe, France" and it'll sit on a sorting table while a human works out which one you meant.

Saint-Sauveur appears 11 times. Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu each appear 10 times. Saint-Marcel, Saint-Michel, Saint-Pierre, and Sainte-Marie each appear 9 times. The top 15 most-repeated names are shown below.

The two-letter club

Below Y's solitary record, thirteen communes hold two-letter names. They are an eclectic group: Eu (Seine-Maritime, population 6,591) is probably the most visited; Gy (Haute-Saône, 1,001) is known to wine merchants; Us (Val-d'Oise, 1,338) is close enough to Paris to make a good trivia answer. Most of these names are survivals — old Celtic, Germanic, or Latin roots that the French administrative machine never lengthened, hyphenated, or sainted.

Lengths from one to 45

Most French commune names fall between 6 and 12 characters. The distribution peaks at 7–8 characters (roughly 3,700 communes each), then thins out gradually — until it suddenly doesn't. The long tail extends past 30 characters because of fusion: when two communes merge administratively, France often concatenates their names with a hyphen rather than choosing one or inventing something new.

The 45-character winner

At the far end of the tail sits the longest commune name in France:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson
Marne (département 51) · population 491 · 45 characters

It contains two saints, three former villages, and a hyphen count that challenges the postal service. The commune in second place — Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur in the Haute-Saône (43 characters) — sounds like someone lost a bet during the 1970s rural fusion drive. The third is Saint-Martin-de-Bienfaite-la-Cressonnière in the Calvados (41 characters), which at least has the grace to make it sound bucolic.

Y and Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson are both real addresses, both administered the same way, both carry the same administrative weight in the French republic. That gap — one character to 45, 89 residents to 491 — is the range of the French commune name in 2023.

A note on the data. Commune names and populations come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which itself references the INSEE commune code table and the 2021 population census. Coverage is 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Name analysis (length, frequency, prefix matching) was computed directly from this file. A small number of communes with null names were excluded.
