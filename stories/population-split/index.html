Half Nation

France has 34,883 communes. Half of all French people live in just 1,021 of them — fewer than 3% of the total. The other 33,862 communes share the rest.

2026-05-08 5 min read

The number is famous: France has around 35,000 communes, more than Germany, Italy, Spain, and Poland combined. It's cited as evidence of a peculiarly French attachment to local governance, a legacy of the Revolutionary decision to transform every parish — however small, however remote — into an administrative unit. The number is real. Less discussed is what it actually implies for where French people live.

Most of those communes are not cities. Most are not even towns. The median French commune — the one at the exact midpoint of the size distribution — has a population of 459. Half of France's 34,883 municipalities are smaller than a mid-size office building.

Three percent

The concentration is more extreme than the median alone suggests. To account for half of France's 67 million residents, you only need the 1,021 largest communes — just 2.9% of the total. The other 33,862 communes together hold the other half.

Go further: just 35 communes — one tenth of one percent — contain 10% of France's population. And just 182 communes contain a quarter of all French people. These are not small cities. Toulouse, at the top of the individual-commune rankings in this dataset, has 504,000 residents. Paris — counted across its 28 arrondissements — adds another 2.2 million. But even below that tier, a dense cluster of cities between 50,000 and 300,000 residents pulls enormous shares of the national population into a very small number of administrative units.

The distribution

The chart below shows France's commune population by size bracket — how many communes fall in each range, and how much of France's total population they account for. The two bars that dominate are the middle ones: the 500–10,000 range holds about 43% of the population across about 15,000 communes. But the rightmost bar — just 154 communes with 50,000 or more residents — accounts for 23% of France all by itself. The leftmost bracket — 3,382 communes with fewer than 100 people — holds 0.3%.

Inequality, measured

Economists measure distributional inequality with the Gini coefficient — a number between 0 (perfect equality) and 1 (total concentration in one place). France's income Gini is around 0.30, in line with most Western European countries. The Gini coefficient for France's commune population distribution is 0.78. By comparison, that's higher than the income inequality of Brazil or South Africa, countries regularly cited as extreme cases. The parallel isn't perfect — income inequality and population concentration are different phenomena — but the number illustrates just how lopsided the spread is.

The chart below is a Lorenz curve: on the x-axis, communes are ordered from smallest to largest; the y-axis shows the cumulative share of France's population they account for. The diagonal dotted line represents perfect equality — where every commune would have the same number of residents. The red curve is the actual data. The further the curve sags below the diagonal, the more unequal the distribution.

The sag is dramatic. At the midpoint of the x-axis — the 50th percentile of communes by size — the curve is at 5.5%. That is, the smallest half of France's communes together hold just 5.5% of its people. The curve does not rise steeply until the last 10–15% of communes, which is where France's cities, suburbs, and towns sit.

The extremes

At one end of the distribution sits Rochefourchat, a commune in the Drôme département (26) with a population of one. Not one thousand. One person. It has a mayor. The position is technically required by law.

Rochefourchat is not unique in kind — it belongs to a category of about 3,382 communes with fewer than 100 residents. Together, all of them hold 0.3% of France's population. Combined, these 3,382 communes have about 219,000 people — fewer than the population of the Paris 15th arrondissement alone.

At the other extreme, Toulouse has 504,078 residents as a single commune — France's largest in this dataset, where Paris is subdivided by arrondissement. Paris itself, aggregated across its 28 arrondissements, is home to 2.18 million, making it roughly equal to the entire population of all of France's smallest 18,000 communes combined.

Why it matters

France's commune structure is not just a curiosity. Each of the 34,883 communes is a legal entity with its own elected mayor and council, its own budget, its own administrative responsibilities. The smallest ones — those with one or five or twenty residents — run elections, maintain registers, manage property, and interact with the prefectural system. A country that governs 34,883 communes is allocating administrative capacity very differently from one that governs 11,000 (Germany) or 8,000 (Spain).

Successive governments have tried to consolidate. The 1971 loi Marcellin offered incentives for communes to merge, but uptake was low: local identity proved more durable than administrative efficiency. The more recent commune nouvelle framework, introduced in 2010 and revised in 2015, has produced a wave of voluntary mergers — several hundred in the 2010s and 2020s. The total commune count has fallen from around 36,700 in the 1960s to about 35,000 today. But the long tail of tiny communes persists. Rochefourchat endures.

A note on the data. Population figures are from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which uses INSEE 2021 census population figures as its base. The dataset covers all 35,014 communes in metropolitan France and overseas territories; 131 entries with population = 0 (primarily the city-level entries for Paris, Lyon, and Marseille, whose populations are recorded at arrondissement level) have been excluded from all calculations here, leaving 34,883 usable commune entries. Paris is counted as 28 separate arrondissements; its total of 2.18 million reflects the sum of those arrondissements. Toulouse (504,078) is the largest single commune unit. The Gini coefficient is calculated using the standard formula on the 34,883 population values.
