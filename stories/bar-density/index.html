The Alps drink more than Paris

49,385 bars — now ranked per capita. Ski resorts dominate; the Paris suburbs turn out to be a bar desert.

2026-05-30 4 min read

Story 4 mapped every one of France's 49,385 active débits de boissons as an amber dot. By raw count, Paris wins: 3,120 bars, more than any other département. Nord (1,961) and Pas-de-Calais (1,265) together edge it out, and Bretagne fields over 3,000 across four départements. Big numbers.

But raw counts reward size. To ask where France actually drinks — where bars are woven most densely into daily life per resident — you need population. The DREES APL dataset gives us commune-level populations for all 34,886 communes in France. Join that to SIRENE bar locations, and the map rewrites itself entirely.

The ski resort anomaly

Among communes with at least 500 residents, the top of the per-capita bar list reads like a ski lift map. Les Deux Alpes: 39 bars, 1,933 residents — 20.2 bars per 1,000 people, or one bar for every 50 residents. Morzine (51 bars, 2,660 residents): 19.2 per 1,000. Val-d'Isère, Courchevel, Tignes, Les Gets — all in the top 15.

Les Deux Alpes has 27× more bars per capita than the national average (7.3 per 10,000). Every winter visitor adds to the headcount at the lift but not to the commune's official population — which stays stubbornly small and year-round.

Corse leads; the IDF suburbs trail

The département picture is steadier — large populations smooth out the tourist spikes — but the story is still surprising. Haute-Corse (2B) leads nationally at 21.1 bars per 10,000 residents. Lozère is second at 17.6 — France's least-populated département, where every village café counts. Corse-du-Sud and Cantal follow. The Alpes cluster: Hautes-Alpes (15.6) and Alpes-de-Haute-Provence (14.6) both beat Paris (75), which lands sixth at 14.6 per 10,000.

The bar desert ring

At the bottom: Essonne (3.1), Hauts-de-Seine (3.3), Yvelines (3.3), Val-d'Oise (3.7), Seine-et-Marne (3.8). All five are IDF suburbs. Together they form the same ring around Paris that appeared in the medical deserts story and the baguettes story. Paris at 14.6 per 10,000 is surrounded by departments at 3–4 — less than a third its density.

These suburbs were built for a different logic: mass housing, commuter rail, retail parks. The village café model that persists in Lozère or Corse never took root here. The diagonale du vide runs empty through the middle of France; the IDF ring is full of people but empty of bars. Two kinds of emptiness, one map.

A note on the data. Bar locations come from INSEE SIRENE (active establishments with NAF code 56.30Z, March 2026 snapshot), geocoded via the SIRENE Géolocalisation file — the same 49,385-point dataset as Story 4. Commune populations come from the DREES APL 2023 file, which uses the INSEE 2021 population base. Commune matching uses the INSEE commune code; 13 bar records (0.03%) could not be matched and were excluded. Per-capita rankings require a minimum commune population of 500 to exclude statistically noisy cases.
