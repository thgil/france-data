The bar capital

Two-thirds of French communes have no bar at all. Adjust for population, and the most bar-dense places in France aren't Paris or Bretagne — they're ski resorts.

2026-05-22 4 min read

France has 49,385 registered bars. That number comes up a lot. But it obscures something: those bars are concentrated in fewer than a third of the country's communes. Two in three French communes — 23,135 out of 34,883 with recorded population — have zero bars. Not one. The café du village is an icon. For most villages, it's gone.

Divide by population

Raw bar counts favour big cities. Nord and Pas-de-Calais together (3,226 bars) edge out Paris (3,121). Bretagne's Finistère (1,014 bars, under a million residents) clearly punches above its weight. But none of that tells you which communities are organised around the bar — where a bar isn't a convenience but a structural feature of daily life, or at least of tourist life.

When you divide bar counts by registered population, the picture shifts completely. The communes at the top aren't the ones you'd expect. The national average is 0.73 bars per 1,000 residents. Paris comes in at 1.46 — twice the average, but hardly remarkable. The real outliers are elsewhere.

It's the Alps

Of the top 17 communes in the ranking, 15 are Alpine ski resorts. Les Deux Alpes leads with 20.2 bars per 1,000 residents — that's 28 times the national average. Morzine (51 bars, 2,660 residents) comes in at 19.2. Courchevel, Val-d'Isère, Tignes, Châtel, Les Gets — the list reads like a piste map.

The logic is simple: ski resorts have bars calibrated for tourist peaks, but population figures reflect only registered residents. A resort town of 2,000 permanent residents hosting 40,000 skiers in January needs infrastructure for the crowd, not the census. SIRENE captures the licences; INSEE captures the locals. The ratio does the rest.

The Pyrenees show up too — Cauterets (Hautes-Pyrénées), Les Angles (Pyrénées-Orientales) — and Dévoluy, a smaller Hautes-Alpes resort, rounds out the mountain bloc. Après-ski isn't just a phrase. It's a business model that shows up in the data.

And then the coast

Beyond the mountain resorts, a second pattern emerges: coastal luxury. Saint-Tropez (34 bars, 3,578 residents, 9.5/1,000) and Deauville (32 bars, 3,565 residents, 9.0/1,000) occupy the same structural position as the ski resorts — tourist-season economies with permanent populations far smaller than their infrastructure suggests. Soorts-Hossegor, Le Touquet-Paris-Plage follow the same logic.

Corsica appears in the mix too, as both Haute-Corse and Corse-du-Sud make the top département list. Rogliano (pop. 540, 7 bars) ranks 9th nationally by commune. Saint-Florent, the island's northern resort town, sits at 9.5/1,000. At département level, Haute-Corse tops France at 2.11 bars per 1,000 — ahead of Lozère (1.76) and Corse-du-Sud (1.69).

Two Breton islands also appear: Île-aux-Moines and La Roche-Bernard — small communities where maritime tourism patterns produce the same surplus-of-bars effect.

Paris registers at 1.46 bars per 1,000 residents. The 1st arrondissement punches highest in Paris, at 7.7/1,000 — a real outlier within the capital, where restaurants and bars cluster around the Marais and Les Halles. But even Paris's busiest arrondissement is half the rate of Morzine or Les Deux Alpes.

The bars story maps where France's 49,385 bars are. This story maps where they're densest relative to who actually lives there. The answer, repeatedly, is: wherever tourists go in winter. The café du village may be dying in rural France. In the mountains, the café is thriving — it just serves a very different clientele than the one in the census register.

A note on the data. Bar counts come from the same SIRENE snapshot used in Story 4 (Where France drinks): 49,385 active établissements with NAF code 56.30Z, geocoded with INSEE's geolocation file (March 2026). Population figures come from the DREES APL 2023 commune-level dataset (census base INSEE 2021), covering 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Bars were matched to communes by INSEE commune code (5-digit code unique identifier); 12 bars (0.02%) could not be matched. The per-capita rankings shown here require at least 500 registered residents to avoid statistical noise from tiny hamlets.
