The village called Y

One letter, thirteen two-letter towns, twelve Sainte-Colombes, and 3,898 saints. A tour of France's 35,000 commune names.

2026-05-13 5 min read

There is a commune in the Somme whose entire official name is the letter Y. Not an abbreviation. Not a postal code. The commune is called Y, population 89, and it has been called Y since at least the Middle Ages. If you write your address as 1 rue de la Paix, 80300 Y, France, that is a valid French postal address. The postman knows where to go.

France has 35,014 communes. Of those, one has a single-letter name. Thirteen more make do with two letters. And then the names bloom — through three-letter villages with names like Gy and Bey and Gex, through hyphenated saints and merged double-villages, all the way to a commune in the Marne whose name runs to 45 characters.

The alphabet in stone

Most of these short names are very old — place names contracted by centuries of spoken use, then frozen in administrative records. Eu in Seine-Maritime was a royal city; Gy in Haute-Saône is ancient enough that its etymology is debated. Oô in Haute-Garonne — two letters, two identical vowels with a circumflex — has the honour of being perhaps the most typographically peculiar commune in France.

Short and very long

The average commune name is 11.8 characters. But the distribution is bimodal — a peak at 7–8 characters for simple ancient names, and a second hump in the 15–20 range from hyphenated compound names. Those longer names usually signal a modern administrative merger: two villages forced together in the 1970s or later, each unwilling to lose its name entirely, so both survive hyphenated into one awkward entity.

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson

Marne (dept. 51) · 45 characters · population 198 · France's longest commune name

The commune in the Marne was created in 1972 from the merger of three villages: Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont, Saint-Genest, and Isson. Three communities, three names, one very long hyphenated result. It is the longest official commune name in France, at 45 characters. For comparison: Y is 1.

Twelve Sainte-Colombes

France has 1,450 commune names that belong to more than one commune — 3,700 communes in total share their name with at least one other. The most duplicated name in France is Sainte-Colombe, with twelve communes. There are eleven Saint-Sauveurs, ten Saint-Aubins. Tell someone you're from Sainte-Colombe and you'll need to follow it with a département.

The saint names dominate because France's communes were largely named in the medieval period, when the local parish church — and its patron saint — was the social anchor of village life. Of the top 20 most common commune names, 15 begin with Saint or Sainte. Overall, 11.1% of all French communes — one in nine — carry a saint's name.

The names are a compressed history: medieval parishes, Occitan contractions, Viking settlements, Roman roads, revolutionary renamings, and mid-century administrative mergers — all fossilised in 35,014 official strings, maintained in a government database, delivered to your browser as a property in a GeoJSON feature. The village named Y has been called Y for over a thousand years. The database just makes it easier to notice.

A note on the data. Commune names and counts are taken from the DREES APL 2023 commune-level dataset, which covers 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Name lengths are computed on the official communeName field as stored in that dataset. The dataset does not include communes déléguées (sub-units of merged communes) separately.
