What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes and only 32,764 unique names. The most common — Sainte-Colombe — appears twelve times, in twelve different départements.

2026-05-28 4 min read

France is a republic of 35,014 communes — more than any other country in the European Union. The smallest has fewer than ten residents; the largest is Paris. Most have names that are entirely their own. But 3,700 communes share their name with at least one other.

Sainte-Colombe, twelve times over

The single most common commune name in France is Sainte-Colombe. There are twelve of them, spread across twelve different départements — from Hautes-Alpes (population 59) to Seine-et-Marne (population 1,793). Together, all twelve Sainte-Colombes hold just 6,605 residents. None of them is particularly famous. Saint Columba — a sixth-century Irish missionary — planted a name that quietly multiplied.

Runner-up is Saint-Sauveur (11 communes), followed by Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu (10 each). The chart below shows the top fifteen.

One in eight communes is a saint

France has 4,437 communes whose name begins with Saint or Sainte — 12.7% of all communes. That is not evenly spread. In the Creuse (département 23, in the Massif Central), 30.6% of communes carry a saint's name. The Ardèche is at 29.3%, the Dordogne at 28.8%. The further south and west you go, the more consecrated the map becomes.

The word Saint appears as a first element more than three times as often as the next most common first word. In the chart below, the dominance is unmistakable.

Y

France's shortest commune name is a single letter: Y. It sits in the Somme, in the north of France, population 89. The name predates the letter itself — it comes from a Gaulish root meaning "water" or "ford." France is also home to By (2 letters), Oz (2), Ry (2), and Uz (2). Beyond those, a further 154 communes have three-letter names.

The long end of the scale

At the other extreme, France's longest commune name is Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson, in the Marne. It runs to 45 characters and 7 hyphens. It contains two saints. It is the result of a 1972 municipal merger that nobody felt like resolving with a shorter name.

Longest names

35,014 communes. 32,764 names. One is a single letter, one runs 45 characters. Nearly 4,500 begin with a saint. The oldest names are Gaulish or Latin; the newest are the results of mid-20th century mergers that appended one name to another with a hyphen and called it done. Together they are an unofficial linguistic atlas of France — not the France of guidebooks, but the France of mairies, road signs, and official correspondence going back a thousand years.

A note on the data. Commune names come from the INSEE APL 2023 commune file, which covers 35,014 communes including metropolitan France and overseas territories. Name counts are case-sensitive except where noted; "Saint" and "Sainte" are counted separately in the prefix analysis. Population figures are from the same file (INSEE 2021 census base). The APL file was first used in Story 2 (medical deserts).
