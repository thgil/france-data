What France is called

35,014 communes. One is named Y. Twelve are named Sainte-Colombe. One has 45 letters.

2026-05-31 5 min read

France's administrative geography is a monument to bureaucratic completeness: 35,014 communes, each with a registered name, a postal code, and an INSEE code. Most names are obvious — Saint-Jean-Baptiste, La Roche-sur-Yon, Les Grandes-Ventes. Some are less obvious. One, in the Somme, is named after a single letter of the alphabet.

Y France's shortest commune name. Population: 89. Département: Somme. INSEE code 80829 · APL score 2.78 (adequate GP access)

Y is not alone in its minimalism. France has 13 communes with two-letter names: By (Doubs), Bû (Eure-et-Loir), Eu (Seine-Maritime, pop. 6,591 — by far the largest of the micro-named), Gy, Oz, Oô, Py, Ri, Ry, Sy, Ur, Us, Uz. Most are tiny villages. Eu is an exception — a proper Norman town that happens to carry a postal stub as its identity.

One in ten communes is a saint

The most common first word in French commune names is not La, not Le, not even a geographic descriptor. It is Saint. 3,587 communes begin with "Saint-"; another 295 begin with "Sainte-". Together, 3,882 communes — 11.1% of the total — are named after Catholic saints.

"La", "Le", and "Les" together name 2,140 communes — geographically descriptive starts that usually signal a natural feature (a meadow, a spring, a hill). "Villers" (105), "Villeneuve" (70), and "Val" (67) follow as markers of settlement patterns. But "Saint" is in a league of its own. France's revolutionary period tried to replace saint-names with civic ones (Saint-Malo briefly became Port-Malo), but the old names returned almost immediately.

Twelve Sainte-Colombes

France has 32,764 unique commune names for 35,014 communes — meaning 3,700 communes share their name with at least one other. There are 1,450 names used by two or more communes. If you addressed a letter to "Sainte-Colombe, France" with no other context, it would theoretically belong in any of twelve different villages — in the Hautes-Alpes, Charente-Maritime, Doubs, Gironde, Ille-et-Vilaine, Landes, Lot, Manche, Rhône, Seine-Maritime, Seine-et-Marne, or Yonne.

The runner-up, Saint-Sauveur, exists in 11 places. Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu tie at 10 each. The top 15 most-duplicated names include 13 saints and 2 descriptive names (Beaulieu — "beautiful place" — and Le Pin). This is not an administrative oversight: medieval France was settled and named long before central coordination existed, and the same religious and geographic vocabulary was used independently across the territory.

45 letters, two villages, one merger

France's longest commune name — at 45 characters — is not a historical quirk. It is an administrative artefact:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson
Marne (dept 51) · population 491 · the result of merging three communes into one

Since the 2010 law creating communes nouvelles, many French villages have merged to avoid administrative dissolution. The naming convention — "A-et-B" or "A-Saint-B-et-C" — strings together the original names rather than inventing a new one. The second-longest name, Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 chars, Haute-Saône), follows the same logic. The top 10 longest commune names are almost all merger artifacts from the last two decades.

The shape of a name

The average French commune name is 11.8 characters long. The distribution peaks at 7–8 characters — the natural length of a "La Roche" or "Montfort" — then extends into a long right tail of hyphenated compound names.

France's naming landscape is a compressed history. Saints mark where medieval parishes stood. "La", "Le", "Les" mark where the land spoke: a meadow, a spring, a stand of pines. "Villers" and "Villeneuve" mark where populations expanded outward from an existing settlement. And the longest names — the Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteurs — mark the 21st century's quiet administrative contractions, two villages legally insisting they are still two villages, even as they share one budget.

And then there is Y. Population 89. In the Somme. No further comment.

A note on the data. Commune names and populations come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which covers 35,014 communes in metropolitan France and overseas territories. Name analysis was performed against the communeName field; length calculations treat the full string including hyphens and spaces. The dataset was originally built for the medical deserts story.
