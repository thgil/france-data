What's in a name?

France has a commune called "Y" — one letter, 89 people. It has 35,014 communes in total, and 11% of them are named after a saint. A tour of the strangest, shortest, and most repeated names in France.

2026-05-14 4 min read

There is a commune in the Somme called Y. Not an abbreviation. Not a nickname. Just the letter Y, chosen at some point in the medieval past and confirmed through every republican reorganisation since. It has a mayor, a church, a post office, and 89 residents. Its postal address works. Its Wikipedia page exists. The commune has, presumably, the shortest street addresses in France: "1 rue de l'Église, Y, 80190."

Y is the only single-letter commune in France. But it is not alone in the compact end of the alphabet. There are 13 communes with two-letter names.

The two-letter club includes Eu (Seine-Maritime, pop 6,591 — named for the river Bresle, not for the European Union, though the mix-up surely happens), Us (Val-d'Oise, pop 1,338), Ry (Seine-Maritime, pop 775 — claimed by some as the model for Flaubert's Madame Bovary), and Oô (Haute-Garonne, pop 103 — two letters, one of which is a vowel with a circumflex). The two shortest place names in England are Ae (Scotland) and Ax (Devon). France has thirteen of them.

The saint hegemony

More than one in nine French commune names begins with Saint- or Sainte-. This is not a coincidence of etymology — it reflects the fact that France was, for most of its administrative history, a Catholic country that named its villages after the feast days of patron saints. The saints were many, the villages were many, and the overlap was inevitable.

The most duplicated commune name in France is Sainte-Colombe, which appears in 12 different départements: Hautes-Alpes, Charente-Maritime, Doubs, Gironde, Ille-et-Vilaine, Landes, Lot, Manche, Rhône, Seine-Maritime, Seine-et-Marne, and Yonne. None of these Sainte-Colombes is the same place. Each has its own mayor. Each observes the feast of Saint Columba. Together, they have a combined population of 6,605.

Saint-Sauveur (11 communes), Saint-Aubin (10), and Beaulieu (10) follow close behind. The deeper you go, the more the list reads as a gazetteer of medieval piety and geography: mont for a hill, beau for a pleasant outlook, saint for the patron. There are 71 communes named Villeneuve (the "new town"), 47 named Neuville (same meaning, different era), 48 named Mont-something. The French countryside is a palimpsest of the same handful of words, laid down over twelve centuries.

At the other extreme

The longest commune name in France is Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson, at 45 characters. It is in the Marne, in the Grand Est region. It was formed in 1973 by the merger of four communes: Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont, Saint-Genest-sur-Isson, and two smaller villages absorbed during the loi Marcellin era of municipal consolidations. The result is a commune whose name describes its own administrative history — two saints, a river valley, and the bureaucratic need to record them all.

Second place goes to Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 characters, Haute-Saône), also a merger. Third: Saint-Martin-de-Bienfaite-la-Cressonnière (41 characters, Calvados). The pattern holds: length is a trace of consolidation. Small communes that were once separate and then joined tend to hyphenate all their old names into one long chain, because to erase a name is to erase an identity. France has 35,014 communes — and a strong institutional preference for keeping all of them.

What do Y and Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson have in common, besides being French? Both are accidents of history. One is a name that survived because it was too old and too established to change. The other is a name that grew because mergers demanded documentation and documentation demanded completeness. The 35,014 communes in between are the same story, told in varying levels of compression.

A note on the data. Commune names and populations come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset (35,014 communes, including overseas territories), which uses INSEE commune codes and names from the 2021 population reference. A companion file — communes-index.json — is exported from this story as a lightweight cross-story reference: 35,014 entries with name, INSEE code, département, population, and bbox centroid.
