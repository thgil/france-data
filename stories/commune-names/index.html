What's in a name

France has 35,014 communes. One is called Y. Eleven percent are Saints. The longest name runs to 45 characters. A tour of the alphabet, one place at a time.

Start with the obvious question: what is France's shortest commune name?

Y

A village of 89 people in the Somme, département 80. Officially styled Y, nothing more. Possibly the shortest municipal name of any country in the world. Its post code is 80190. The café is called, inevitably, Bar de Y.

Y isn't alone at the short end. France has 13 two-letter communes: Bû (Eure-et-Loir, pop. 2,022), Sy (Ardennes, pop. 54), Oô (Haute-Garonne, pop. 103 — those two vowels with circumflexes are doing a lot of work), Oz, Ri, Ry, Py, Ur, Uz, By, Eu, Gy, Us. Most are tiny. All are memorable.

The empire of Saints

The most striking fact about French commune names isn't any individual oddity — it's a structural one. 3,882 communes (11.1%) begin with Saint- or Sainte-. More than one in nine French places is named after a Catholic saint.

Saint-Martin leads the field with 202 communes — there are more Saint-Martins in France than there are communes in many smaller European countries. Saint-Jean follows with 155, then Saint-Pierre (144), Saint-Germain (108), Saint-Julien (78), Saint-Laurent (77). The pattern isn't coincidence: the medieval church organised French territory, and saint names were the obvious way to distinguish one village from the next in adjacent valleys.

Because of the Saints, the letter S absolutely dominates the French commune alphabet. 5,598 communes start with S — far more than any other letter. L is second with 4,279 (a mix of Saints-that-dropped-the-hyphen and the article Le / La / Les, which appears in 2,140 commune names). B and C cluster in third, driven by the deep inventory of Bois-de-…, Beau-…, Château-…, and Chemin-… names.

At the other extreme: 45 characters

If Y is the shortest, what's the longest? That honour goes to a commune in the Marne:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson

Forty-five characters. Population 491. The name dates from a 1972 municipal merger that joined three villages but refused to let any of them disappear entirely. The same logic explains the second-longest: Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 chars, Haute-Saône), and Saint-Martin-de-Bienfaite-la-Cressonnière (41, Calvados) — every hyphen is a negotiation.

Most names cluster around seven characters

Name lengths follow an asymmetric bell curve: the most common length is 7–8 characters, the peak of the distribution. Very short names are rare (there's only one at 1 character, 13 at 2). Very long names also thin out but trail further: a significant tail of merged communes runs up to 45 characters. The median is around 11–12 characters — roughly the length of Saint-Martin or Villeneuve.

The margins of the alphabet

Some letters are almost absent. X has just 21 communes, concentrated in the south-west — Gascony and Basque country, where Occitan and Basque roots produce endings in -ax, -ox, -ux: Xaintrailles, Xaintray, Xonrupt-Longemer.

Z has 34 communes, almost entirely in Alsace: Zaessingue, Zarbeling, Zegerscappel, Zellenberg, Zermezeele. These are Germanic or Alsatian in origin — -kappel (chapel), -berg (hill), -mühle (mill) — remnants of the centuries when the province changed hands between France and Germany. Alsace rejoined France in 1918 and again in 1945, but the Z communes stayed Z.

K has 77 communes, mostly in Brittany (Kervignac, Ploëzal) and Alsace (Kintzheim, Krautergersheim). The K communes are a linguistic map: wherever you find them, you're touching the edge of France where a different language — Breton, Alsatian — shaped the place before French did.

A census in names

A commune name is almost impossible to change. It requires a prefectoral decree, a vote, and a compelling administrative reason. The result is that France carries its medieval geography as a live data field — every saint cult, every feudal land grant, every linguistic border encoded in 35,014 names that haven't changed much since Napoleon standardised the commune system in 1789.

Y is still Y. Saint-Martin is still 202 villages. And somewhere in the Marne, a postman still has to stamp Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson on an envelope.

A note on the data. Commune names and population figures come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset (data.gouv.fr), which covers 35,014 communes including overseas territories. The population field is based on INSEE 2021 census figures as used in the APL model. The communes-index.json derived from this dataset is reused for name-based analysis here; the underlying APL scores power Story 2 (medical deserts). Letter counts are normalised so that accented initial capitals (É, Î, Ô, etc.) are folded into their base letter for the chart.
