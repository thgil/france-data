What's in a name?

35,014 communes, 32,764 unique names — and one called just Y.

2026-06-03 4 min read

France has 35,014 communes. Most have sensible names — Lyon, Bordeaux, Saint-Pierre-du-Mont. And then there is one commune whose entire official name is a single letter.

Y

Commune of Y, Somme (département 80). Population: 89.

Y is not an abbreviation. It is not short for anything. The commune of Y sits in the Somme valley about 25 km southeast of Amiens, and its name has been Y since at least the 12th century — derived from a Germanic root for water or low-lying land, cognate with the Dutch aa. There is no record of anyone finding this unusual.

Y is not alone

Y may be exceptional, but two-letter communes are not. France has 13 of them:

Most are ancient Celtic or Germanic roots: Eu (Seine-Maritime) was a royal residence; Gy (Haute-Saône) dates to Roman times; Oz (Isère) is a ski station; Oô (Haute-Garonne) sits at the foot of the Pyrenees. The two-letter club spans the whole country.

One in nine is a Saint

11.1% of French communes — one in nine — begin with Saint or Sainte. That's 3,898 communes, honouring hundreds of different saints with wildly varying degrees of fame. Sainte-Colombe (12 occurrences) is the most common, spread across 12 separate départements with no connection to each other beyond veneration of a 3rd-century martyr. Saint-Sauveur appears 11 times; Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu tie at 10.

And then the saints compound. The longest commune name in France — at 45 characters — manages to pack two of them in:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson
Marne (département 51) — 45 characters — population 278

This name is the product of a 1972 merger of several villages, a French administrative tradition that has given the country some of its more memorable strings of hyphens.

The hyphen nation

38.3% of French communes have hyphenated names — 13,410 out of 35,014. The hyphen does a lot of work in French toponymy: it joins directional qualifiers (-sur-Loire, -sous-Bois, -lès-Metz), merged village names, saint names, and the occasional particle. Strip the hyphens and you lose most of the geography.

Most common commune names (top 20)

The top of the duplicate list is dominated by saints and pleasant-sounding adjectives. Beaulieu ("beautiful place") appears 10 times. Le Pin ("the pine") 9 times. Beaumont, Verrières, Fleury, Brion — each shows up across handfuls of unrelated départements, given independently to communities that presumably all had a beautiful hill or a row of pines.

How long is a commune name?

The median commune name is 9 characters. The distribution peaks between 6 and 10 characters and has a long right tail — the merged-village names running out past 30 characters. Y anchors the left end alone.

Commune name lengths

Commune names are sediment. They preserve a Roman road junction, a forest clearing, a saint whose feast day someone remembered, a merger nobody wanted. France's administrative map is a palimpsest — the 35,014 names written over centuries of movement, devotion, and paperwork. Y is just the most compressed version of that history.

A note on the data. Commune names and populations come from the medical-deserts story dataset: DREES APL 2023, joined to IGN commune boundaries, covering 35,014 communes in metropolitan France and overseas territories.
