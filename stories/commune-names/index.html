What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes — and one of them is named Y. A tour through the country's strangest, most duplicated, and longest place names.

2026-05-14 5 min read

France has more communes than any other country in the European Union — 35,014 of them, compared to Germany's roughly 11,000 or Spain's 8,000. Each one carries a name that was fixed in law, contested in courts, or simply inherited from a medieval village register. Those 35,014 names form a kind of national vocabulary, and like any good dictionary, it has outliers: the very short, the very long, and the suspiciously repeated.

The shortest name in France

The commune with the shortest name in France is not a typo.

Y Département de la Somme (80) · Population: 89 · Founded: before the 12th century

Y is a village in Picardy, 25 kilometres north-east of Compiègne. Its name — a single letter — derives from a Gallo-Roman root, likely related to water or a nearby estate. For centuries it has confused postal services, navigation systems, and anyone trying to Google it. It is the only commune in France with a one-character name.

The most common name: Sainte-Colombe

At the other end of the uniqueness scale: Sainte-Colombe. There are twelve of them. Not twelve variants — twelve communes with exactly this name, spread across twelve différent départements from the Hautes-Alpes (05) to Seine-et-Marne (77).

Sainte Colombe of Sens was a 3rd-century martyr; her cult spread along early Christian trade routes. Every town along those routes that built a chapel in her name, and later became a commune, is now one of the twelve. Finding the right Sainte-Colombe on a French administrative form requires a département code — the name alone is not enough.

Saint-Sauveur comes second with 11 occurrences. Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu share third place with 10 each.

One in nine communes is a saint

The dominance of Saint- and Sainte- as commune-name prefixes isn't just cultural colour — it's structural. Out of 35,014 communes, 3,898 start with Saint or Sainte. That's 11.1% — one in every nine French communes carries a canonised patron.

No other prefix comes close. La Chapelle is the next most common first element, at 147 communes — a distant second. Villers follows at 103. The chart below shows the top twelve prefixes.

This pattern is a direct map of medieval parish geography. Every commune whose name begins with Saint- was once a parish church: the commune that grew around it inherited the dedication. France's administrative map is, in this sense, a palimpsest of the Church's own territorial grid.

45 characters

The longest commune name in France belongs to a village in the Marne:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson

Forty-five characters. The name is the result of a 1972 merger of three separate communes — Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont, Saint-Genest, and Isson — hyphenated together into a single administrative entity. It has 342 residents. It is not the easiest commune to write on an envelope.

The runners-up are no less baroque: Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 chars, Haute-Saône), Saint-Martin-de-Bienfaite-la-Cressonnière (41 chars, Calvados), and Roche-sur-Linotte-et-Sorans-les-Cordiers (40 chars, Haute-Saône). Each one a merger preserved in amber.

1,450 shared names

In total, 1,450 commune names are shared by two or more municipalities. That's 3,700 communes — 10.6% of the total — that must qualify their address with a département code to be unambiguous. In the age of GPS and postal codes this is merely inconvenient. For 19th-century civil servants drawing up the first état-civil registers, it was a genuine administrative problem — one they solved by never quite solving it.

The pattern is almost entirely saints and geography: saints because medieval parishes spread identical dedications across trade routes; geography because every ridge has a village called Beaumont (beautiful hill), every river crossing a village called Pont-something, every clearing a Villeneuve (new town). The names weren't invented — they described. And France had a lot of the same landscape to describe.

A note on the data. Commune names and codes are drawn from the DREES APL 2023 commune dataset, which includes 35,014 communes across metropolitan France and overseas territories.
