Y is a village

35,014 commune names: France's shortest, most repeated, and longest place names decoded.

2026-05-30 5 min read

France has 35,014 communes. Each one has a name — from the terse to the baroque, from the universal to the utterly unique. When you look at all 35,014 at once, patterns emerge that no map can show: names that repeat across a dozen départements, names shorter than a text abbreviation, names so long they've been condensed by habit before they're ever written down.

One letter. That's the whole name.

In the Somme, there is a commune called Y. Not Y-something — just Y. Population 89. Founded in Carolingian times, with a Gothic church and a market square. The name likely derives from a Germanic word for water. It doesn't matter: it's one letter, and it's official.

Y is the extreme case, but France has thirteen more communes with only two letters in their name. Eu (Seine-Maritime, 6,591 residents) is the best-known — a medieval port town where William the Conqueror married. Uz (Hautes-Pyrénées, 36 residents) is the smallest. Oô (Haute-Garonne, 103 residents) is the most typographically distinctive. Together, these fourteen one-and-two-letter communes contain 11,682 people who give their address in fewer characters than most passwords.

Sainte-Colombe: twelve times over

Of France's 35,014 communes, only 32,764 have distinct names — meaning 2,250 communes share their name with at least one other. At the top sits Sainte-Colombe, which appears twelve times, spread across twelve different départements from Charente-Maritime to the Rhône. When you send a letter to Sainte-Colombe, you need the postal code. The name alone is not enough.

Saint-Sauveur appears eleven times. Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu appear ten times each. The pattern reflects how France was named: locally, independently, by parish communities with no coordination and the same stock of saintly and natural vocabulary. Saint-Sauveur is a holy name. Beaulieu means "beautiful place." These names were coined in hundreds of valleys by thousands of priests and village councils over a thousand years, and they converged.

Eleven percent of France is a saint

Saint-* and Sainte-* communes account for 3,882 of France's 35,014 communes — 11.1% of the total. Add Le, La, and Les prefixes (2,140 communes, 6.1%) and you've named nearly one in five communes with just five words. The village vocabulary of France was not diverse. It was sacred, topographic, and deeply repetitive.

The longest name is forty-five characters

At the other extreme sits a Marne commune that is, by some margin, France's longest official place name:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson 45 characters · Marne (51) · population 280

The name is a hyphenated merger of three former communes — Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont, Saint-Genest, and Isson — fused in 1972. French commune mergers often produce names like this: two or three names joined by hyphens and et, preserving every part for historical continuity, regardless of what the result sounds like. The second-longest, Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 chars, Haute-Saône), follows the same logic. These are administrative artifacts more than living names.

France has merged ~2,500 communes since the 1970s under the loi Marcellin. Many mergers are preserved in hyphenated names. The longest names are almost always fusions — places that refused to disappear entirely, so they became part of a longer name instead.

France's commune names are a compressed thousand-year history: the saints, the hills, the rivers, the Latin, the Frankish, the Breton, the Occitan. Y and Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson both sit in the same national registry, assigned the same type of INSEE code, governed by the same administrative logic. One is nine syllables. The other is one letter. Both are France.

A note on the data. All commune names and codes come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset joined to the IGN/Etalab administrative boundaries (DS-CONTOURS), which provides 35,014 commune records including overseas territories. Populations are from the INSEE 2021 census base embedded in that file. Name analysis was performed against the communeName field in that dataset. The communes-index.json file generated for this story (35,014 entries, ~3 MB) is used as a shared data asset by other stories on this site.
