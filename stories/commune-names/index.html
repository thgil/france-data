What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes — but only 32,764 unique names. The shortest is one letter. Twelve towns share the same name. Over 3,800 are saints.

2026-05-31 4 min read

Every French commune has an official name registered with INSEE. There are 35,014 of them. That sounds like 35,014 distinct names — but it isn't. Only 32,764 are unique. The other 2,250 are shared: the same name appearing in two, three, or twelve different places simultaneously.

89% of names — 31,314 — appear exactly once, attached to a single commune in a single département. The remaining 11% are contested geography: a name claimed by several patches of land scattered across France, each with its own history, its own prefect, its own town hall.

One letter is enough

The shortest commune name in France is Y. Not a placeholder. Not a footnote. The commune of Y (pronounced roughly like the English letter "E") sits in the Somme, département 80, population 89. It has a mairie. It has a postal code. It has existed under this name for centuries.

Y Commune of Y, Somme (dept. 80) — population 89. The shortest official commune name in France.

Y is not alone in brevity. Thirteen more communes carry names of just two characters — Sy, By, Bû, Oô (two letters, both with accent), Eu in Seine-Maritime, Gy in Haute-Saône. French toponymy, at its tersest, doesn't bother with syllables.

Twelve Sainte-Colombes

At the other end of the frequency scale sits Sainte-Colombe — the single most duplicated commune name in France. There are twelve of them, spread across twelve different départements: from Charente-Maritime to Seine-et-Marne, from Finistère to Yonne. Each one is named for the third-century martyr Columba of Sens. Each one is its own commune, its own budget, its own mayor. Sainte-Colombe (Rhône, population 1,953) is the largest; Sainte-Colombe (Hautes-Alpes, population 59) is the smallest.

The saint network

France is, topographically speaking, a nation of saints. Of its 35,014 communes, 3,882 — more than one in ten — begin with Saint- or Sainte-. Add the variants that drop the hyphen or use the older spelling St., and the count climbs further. The first word of a commune name is Saint in 3,596 cases and Sainte in 295 more.

After the saints come the landscapes: La (1,054 communes), Le (758), Les (328). Then the patterns that signal geography — Villers (105), a Germanic toponym meaning "settlement", and Villeneuve (70), the medieval new town. Pont (63) marks a bridge. Fontaine (58) marks a spring. The maps of France's communes are also maps of its medieval economy.

45 characters and two saints

The longest commune name is Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson — 45 characters, two saints, one conjunction, one town in the Marne that got there by a series of administrative mergers. Commune fusion in France often works by concatenation: two communes join, and their names join too, hyphenated and preserved. The result is a running archive of which places once existed as independent communes and which no longer do.

Runner-up: Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur in Haute-Saône, at 43 characters — another fusion, another clause in the administrative record.

A note on the data. Commune names and INSEE codes come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which covers all 34,886 communes in metropolitan France and overseas territories with APL scores. This story uses 35,014 records — the full DREES dataset including communes without APL scores. Centroids are computed as simple polygon-mean coordinates from the 1000m-simplified administrative boundaries (IGN/Etalab, 2025 vintage). Population figures are INSEE 2021 base.
