What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes. They share twelve Sainte-Colombes, 4,437 saints, and one simply called Y.

2026-05-25 4 min read

Every French commune has a name. Some are poetic, some are practical, some are just a saint's name followed by a cardinal direction. And then there are the outliers — the ones that are genuinely strange, and the ones that are so common they exist twelve times over across the country, each convinced it's the original.

The APL health-access dataset covers 35,014 communes from the Ardennes to Guyane. What follows is what their names reveal.

Y

There is a commune called Y. It sits in the Somme département, about 20 km northeast of Saint-Quentin. Its population is 89. Its postal code is 80190. Its official INSEE code is 80829. It has one letter, which is unusual even for a country with no shortage of short names.

Two-letter communes tend to be very small. Uz (Hautes-Pyrénées) has 36 residents. Sy (Ardennes) has 54. Oô (Haute-Garonne) — which looks like a face but is pronounced something like "oh-oh" — has 103. The largest is Eu (Seine-Maritime), a proper town with 6,591 residents and a royal château where Queen Victoria once stayed.

Twelve Sainte-Colombes

The most common commune name in France is Sainte-Colombe, which appears 12 times — in the Hautes-Alpes, in the Charente-Maritime, in the Doubs, in the Gironde, in Ille-et-Vilaine, in the Landes, in the Lot, in the Manche, in the Rhône, in the Seine-Maritime, in the Seine-et-Marne, and in the Yonne. Combined population: about 6,600 people. None of them is particularly close to any of the others.

The saint economy

4,437 communes — 12.7% of all French communes — carry "Saint" or "Sainte" in their name. They are home to 7.4 million people. This is the legacy of medieval parish naming, when a commune's identity was its patron saint, and there was rarely a reason to be original about it.

The letter S starts more commune names than any other, with 5,598 — and Saint/Sainte accounts for the vast majority of those. The next most common starting letters are L (4,279 — for Le, La, Les), C (3,696), B (3,190), and M (3,152).

The shape of French commune names

The average commune name is 11.8 characters long. The distribution is right-skewed — most names cluster between 6 and 18 characters, with a long tail of hyphenated compound names that grow by accretion. The longest, at 45 characters, is Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson in the Marne, which was formed by merging several villages and decided to keep everyone's name.

The name of a French commune is not just an address — it's a compressed history. A saint. A river. A hill. A merger forced by administrative convenience. France has been reorganizing its communes since at least Napoleon, and every fusion leaves its trace: a hyphen, a conjunction, a name that sounds like it was written by committee, because it was.

Somewhere in the Somme, a village of 89 people answers to a single letter. Somewhere in the Marne, a commune of 491 carries 45 of them. Both are France. Both are on the map. Both have a bus stop.

A note on the data. Name analysis is based on the communeName field from the DREES APL 2023 commune-level dataset, which covers 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Name strings use INSEE's official romanized forms. Some very small communes or recently merged communes may have variant spellings in other datasets.
