What's in a name?

35,014 communes. One is called Y. Twelve are called Sainte-Colombe. A third have a hyphen. The shortest is one letter; the longest is 45.

2026-06-01 5 min read

France has 35,014 communes. Most have perfectly ordinary names — La Roche-sur-Yon, Montpellier, Saint-Denis. Some do not. There is a commune in the Somme with a population of 89, a small mairie, and a name that raises eyebrows whenever someone types it into a form: Y.

Just Y. The twenty-fifth letter of the alphabet. The shortest official commune name in France, and almost certainly in Europe. It appears on tax documents, on road signs, in the national census — one letter, standing alone.

Y is an outlier, but it has company. Thirteen communes in France have two-letter names: By (Doubs), Eu (Seine-Maritime), Gy (Haute-Saône), Oz (Isère), Ur (Pyrénées-Orientales), Us (Val-d'Oise), and seven others. These are not modern administrative inventions — they are medieval names worn down by centuries of use, their vowels and consonants eroding like stone.

The shape of French place names

Across all 35,014 communes, the average name is 11.8 characters long and the median is 10. The distribution has a clean peak around 7–8 characters and a long right tail stretching past 40. Most communes fall in the comfortable middle — a short article (Le, La, Les), a geographic feature, done. The outliers at both ends are worth lingering on.

At the far right sits Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson (Marne), at 45 characters and seven hyphens. It was assembled in 1972 by merging three smaller communes. The runner-up, Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (Haute-Saône, 43 characters), was similarly consolidated. Long names are almost always merger scars — two or three old villages stitched into one administrative unit, each unwilling to lose its name entirely.

The saint invasion

3,882 commune names — roughly one in nine — begin with Saint- or Sainte-. France's devotion to its saints is written into the map as literally as it gets. Saint-Martin alone accounts for 202 communes. Saint-Jean follows with 155. Saint-Pierre with 144. These aren't just religious dedications: they're the default naming convention of medieval parish geography, repeated wherever a new settlement needed a patron.

The most duplicated exact commune name in France is Sainte-Colombe, which appears 12 times — in the Hautes-Alpes, Charente-Maritime, Doubs, Gironde, Ille-et-Vilaine, Landes, Lot, Manche, Rhône, Seine-Maritime, Seine-et-Marne, and Yonne. Twelve separate communes, twelve separate mairies, twelve sets of residents, all officially sharing the same name. When you mail something to Sainte-Colombe, the postcode is what saves you.

The hyphen republic

13,410 communes — 38% of the total — contain at least one hyphen in their official name. France is, by this measure, the most hyphenated country in Europe. The hyphen is doing real administrative work: it's how the French commune system absorbs complexity, merges histories, and avoids choosing between Saint-Laurent and les-Bains or Laval-d'Aurelle. The result is a nation where a postman needs a good memory.

835 communes have an apostrophe. 108 have a cédille. 1,054 begin with La, 838 with Le, 328 with Les. The definite article is everywhere — France names its places the way it names its dishes, with the article built in and non-negotiable.

Every commune name is a compressed history lesson. Y is Hy minus a century of phonetic drift. Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson is three villages that couldn't agree on which name to lose. Sainte-Colombe is a medieval dedication so common that it spread across twelve departments and became, by accident, a nationwide landmark.

The form on which you enter a French commune name needs a long field. The data confirms it.

A note on the data. Commune names and centroids are derived from the DREES APL 2023 commune-level dataset, which covers all 35,014 communes in metropolitan France and overseas territories. Name analysis was performed on the communeName field. Population figures are from the INSEE 2021 base. Centroid coordinates approximate the bounding-box centre of each commune polygon.
