What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes, with names from one letter to 45. One in eight is named for a saint. And twelve separate villages are all called Sainte-Colombe.

2026-05-24 5 min read

In the Somme, ninety people live in a commune with a single-letter name: Y. There's a post office, a church, a road sign that just says Y. The name predates the written record — it's thought to come from a pre-Gallo-Roman root meaning water or ford, which is how most extremely short French place names got that way.

At the other end of the alphabet, in the Marne, a village of 491 people has spent centuries answering the question: "Where exactly?" with:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson 45 characters · Marne (51) · pop. 491

Between Y and that name lies the whole taxonomy of French geography: saints and streams, forests and fortresses, mergers and memory.

A right-skewed alphabet

The median commune name is 8 characters long — a crisp, two-syllable word like Lormont or Altkirch. But the distribution has a long tail. Names shorter than five characters are rare (888 communes), while names longer than 20 characters number over 3,700. The long names are almost always the result of fusions: two communes forced together by administrative reform, each insisting its name survive, joined forever by a hyphen that reads like a compromise.

The very short: Gy, Py, Uz

After Y, thirteen communes have two-letter names. Most are in eastern France — Normandy, Franche-Comté, the Pyrenees — where pre-Latin and Germanic roots survived the Romanisation that lengthened most Gaulish place names. Eu (Seine-Maritime, pop. 6,591) is the largest two-letter commune, a Norman port town with a château where Queen Victoria once visited. Oô (Haute-Garonne) has two vowels and no consonants, and is in the mountains.

The saint epidemic

France named 4,410 communes for saints — 12.6% of the total. Saint-Martin is the undisputed champion: 249 communes carry the name of the Roman legionary turned Bishop of Tours. That's 249 different places in 32 different départements, all with the same name. If you drove to each one in order, you'd cross the country twice.

Saint Jean (169), Saint Pierre (163), and Saint Germain (120) follow. The top twelve saints together account for 1,416 communes. Christianity built France's map.

Twelve Sainte-Colombes

6.4% of communes share their name with at least one other commune. Sainte-Colombe appears twelve times — scattered across twelve departments from Hautes-Alpes to Yonne. Saint-Sauveur eleven times. Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu ten times each. This is why every French commune has an INSEE code, and why every local knows to say not "I'm from Saint-Marcel" but "I'm from Saint-Marcel-en-Marcillat."

Everything flows to the river

1,991 communes have -sur- in their name: sur la Loire, sur la Seine, sur Mer, sur Saône. Water defined territory long before roads or railways. A commune "sur" something usually means it was a crossing point, a mill, a ford — the reason the village existed at all. Chennevières-sur-Marne, Conflans-Sainte-Honorine-sur-Seine, Villeneuve-sur-Lot: the river is the address.

Another 2,220 communes start with Le, La, Les, or L' — the definite article, marking the commune as a place that needed no qualifier. La Chapelle appears four times. Le Pin nine times. Les Essarts five. They all mean, essentially: you know the one.

Names as memory

French commune names are a compressed history of the country. Saints mark the reach of the medieval church. -sur- and -en- encode the geography that mattered before maps. The long hyphenated names record every merger, every administrative reshuffling, every village that refused to disappear into a number. And somewhere in the Somme, ninety people live in the shortest-named commune on the map — Y — which is one letter and probably all the history anyone needs.

A note on the data. Commune names, codes, populations, and centroids come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset joined to administrative boundaries (Etalab/data.gouv.fr contours communes 2025). The dataset covers 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Name lengths are computed on the official communeName field from INSEE. A small number of communes with null names are excluded.
