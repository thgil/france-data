What's in a name?

France has a commune called Y. It also has one called Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson. Between those two extremes sit 35,014 names — and they reveal a lot about how France was made.

2026-05-23 4 min read

Y is a commune in the Somme. Population: 89. It has a church, a mairie, a post office that has delivered mail addressed simply to "Y, 80190" since before the Revolution. It is, by one measure, the simplest place in France to write a postcard to.

At the other end sits Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson, a commune in the Marne with 491 residents and a name that runs to 45 characters, three saints, and the kind of hyphenation that suggests several villages once decided to resolve a border dispute by merging and refusing to drop anyone's name.

A bimodal distribution

Plot all 35,014 commune name lengths and something interesting appears: the distribution has two humps. The first peaks at 7–8 characters — Nantes, Rennes, Troyes, old established cities with short medieval names. Then there's a long tail, and a second, broader mound around 16–22 characters. That second hump is the fingerprint of the 19th century: when small communes merged, they often hyphenated their names together, doubling or tripling the character count in one administrative stroke.

The alphabet's edge

Y is alone at one letter, but 13 communes manage it in two. Some are ancient Celtic or Latin roots worn smooth by a thousand years of usage: Eu (Seine-Maritime, 6,591 residents) takes its name from the river Bresle's old name and is famous for its château. Gy (Haute-Saône, 1,001 residents) is a wine commune. Bû (Eure-et-Loir, 2,022 residents) keeps its circumflex against all odds. Oô (Haute-Garonne, 103 residents) looks like an owl. All fourteen one- and two-letter communes are genuine, occupied, tax-paying places.

The republic of saints

France has no official state religion since 1905 — but its map didn't get the memo. Of the country's 35,014 communes, 3,898 begin with Saint or Sainte. That's 11.1% of the entire country, a republic of saints larger than most European nations' administrative districts.

After Saint come the articles: La (1,054), Le (758), Les (328). Then the repeated placenames: Villers (105 communes), Villeneuve (70), Val (67), Pont (63). France is a country of villages at rivers (pont = bridge), new towns (villeneuve), and old manors (villers from Latin villare). The prefix map is a compressed history of how France was settled.

Twelve Sainte-Colombes

The most duplicated commune name in France is Sainte-Colombe — there are twelve of them, scattered across twelve different départements, none aware that eleven others share their name. Saint-Sauveur appears eleven times. Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu each appear ten times. None of these duplications are accidents; they reflect the deep pattern of local toponymy, where the same saint or the same geographic feature (the beautiful view, the good water, the old bridge) got named independently, a thousand times over.

The map is a manuscript

France has been continuously inhabited, administered, and renamed for two millennia. The commune name list is the residue: Celtic rivers, Roman villas, Frankish saints, medieval lords, revolutionary renaming, and 19th-century mergers all visible in the same dataset. Y was already Y when the Revolution tried to rationalize French administration. Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson became itself in 1972 when three communes decided that was better than picking one name over the others.

Every name is a sediment layer. The map of France, read at the commune level, is a manuscript written over two thousand years by people who mostly just wanted to name where they lived.

A note on the data. Commune names and populations come from the medical deserts dataset — the DREES APL 2023 file joined to INSEE commune boundaries, which covers 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Name length counts include the full official name as registered by INSEE, including hyphens and accented characters. The commune "Y" (code 80829, Somme) has been verified against the official INSEE commune register.
