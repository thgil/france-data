What's in a Name?

35,014 French communes, 32,764 distinct names — from Y (pop. 89) to a 45-letter hyphenated behemoth, and 3,898 saints in between.

2026-05-23 4 min read

France has 35,014 communes. Most have unremarkable names — a saint, a hill, a river crossing. But open the list and the edges get strange fast. There is a commune in the Somme with a population of 89 and an entirely valid INSEE code. Its name is a single letter.

Y

That's it. Just Y. Commune code 80829, département Somme (80), population 89. Not an abbreviation, not a placeholder — Y is the official registered name of a French commune, and has been for centuries. It earns its own entry in the national address database, its own APL score (2.78 — not a medical desert), its own place on the map.

Note Oô, in Haute-Garonne — two letters, both circular. And Eu, in Seine-Maritime, which has the distinction of being both a commune name and a Latin preposition. These names are not whimsy; they are mostly relics of very old, pre-Romance languages — Celtic, Norse, Basque, or simply truncated over centuries of administrative copying.

A nation of saints

Move from the shortest names to the most common, and a pattern emerges immediately: France is a country of saints. Of the 35,014 communes, 3,898 — 11.1% — carry a name beginning with Saint- or Sainte-. That's 3,587 masculine and 295 feminine. If all the Saints seceded, they would form a nation of roughly 6.6 million people.

The single most common name in France is Sainte-Colombe — shared by exactly 12 communes in 12 different départements: Hautes-Alpes, Charente-Maritime, Doubs, Gironde, Ille-et-Vilaine, Landes, Lot, Manche, Rhône, Seine-Maritime, Seine-et-Marne, and Yonne. Second is Saint-Sauveur at 11, then Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu tied at 10.

The name collision problem

France's 35,014 communes share only 32,764 distinct names — meaning 3,700 communes share their name with at least one other. If you live in Saint-Aubin, your postman relies on your département. This is precisely why France invented the INSEE commune code in the 1940s: a five-digit national identifier, immune to naming ambiguity. Every Sainte-Colombe gets a unique five-digit code. The name is poetry; the code is administration.

The administrative epic

At the other extreme sits France's longest commune name: 45 characters, rendered here at whatever font size your screen allows:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson 45 characters · Marne · pop. 491

This name is not random length. Most of France's longest commune names result from administrative fusions — two or more small communes merged by prefectoral decree, their names hyphenated together in full. The Marne holds the record, with Haute-Saône a close rival (two entries in the top six, totalling 43 and 40 characters). The longest names read less like place names and more like municipal committee decisions, which is essentially what they are.

The shape of the corpus

Most commune names cluster between 6 and 12 characters. The distribution peaks at 7–8, falls off through the teens, then thins to a long rightward tail — the hyphenated fusions that stretch to 30, 40, 45 characters. The median is 10 characters; the mean is 11.8. One commune has 1. One has 45.

Names encode centuries of religious devotion, landscape memory, and administrative improvisation. The saints dominate because medieval parish geography was the first national grid. The duplicates proliferate because every valley had its own saint and its own prefect. The long names record post-war rationalisations nobody quite finished. And one commune in the Somme is just Y, and has been for longer than France has been France.

A note on the data. Commune names, INSEE codes, département codes, and populations come from the APL 2023 commune dataset published by DREES, which covers 35,014 communes including overseas territories. The APL file was already in this repository from the medical deserts story. No new datasets were downloaded for this story.
