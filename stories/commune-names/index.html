35,014 ways to name a place

From a single letter to 45 characters — France's commune names are a cartographic accident, a saint's calendar, and a merger ledger all at once.

2026-05-30 5 min read

France has 35,014 communes. Each one has an official name. The shortest is one letter long.

Y is a village in the Somme, département 80, population 89. It sits on a gentle plateau north-east of Paris, a few kilometres from the Somme river. There is no mystery about how it got its name — it comes from a Gaulish root meaning water or river — but the result is the most abbreviated place name in France. One keystroke. One letter. A village.

Just below Y, 13 communes make do with two letters. They form a scattered alphabet: By, Bû, Eu, Gy, Oz, Oô, Py, Ri, Ry, Sy, Ur, Us, Uz. Short names cluster in old parts of France — the Somme, Normandie, the Pyrénées — where ancient settlement names never got the later Latinate or Frankish padding that lengthened names elsewhere.

The saint epidemic

3,898 communes — one in nine — have names beginning with Saint or Sainte. That is not a quirk; it is the sediment of a thousand years of parish naming. When a settlement formed around a church, it took the church's patron saint as its identifier. The patron became the address.

Saint-Martin leads: 202 communes bear that name, spread across every corner of France from the Pas-de-Calais to the Var, from Finistère to Alsace. Saint-Jean follows with 155, then Saint-Pierre (144), Saint-Germain (108), Saint-Julien (78). The Catholic calendar is also a density map.

Most common saints in commune names (top 12)

The most common full commune name — the identical string repeated most often — is Sainte-Colombe, which appears 12 times. Twelve different villages, twelve different départements, one name. Saint-Sauveur appears 11 times; Saint-Aubin 10 times. France has 1,450 distinct names shared by at least two communes, covering 3,700 communes in total. This means that if you name any of these communes, you are also — technically — naming somewhere else.

The merger beasts

The longest commune name in France belongs to a village in the Marne:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson 45 characters · Dept. 51 (Marne) · formed by merger

It is 45 characters, three hyphenated place-names in one. Long commune names in France are almost always merger artifacts. When communes fuse administratively — usually to pool services, schools, or mairies — the result sometimes keeps both names joined by a hyphen, an et, or a preposition. The top of the length ranking reads like a list of bureaucratic compromises: Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 characters), Saint-Martin-de-Bienfaite-la-Cressonnière (41), Montigny-Mornay-Villeneuve-sur-Vingeanne (40).

The bell curve of place names

Most commune names fall between 6 and 18 characters, with a peak at 7–8. The average is 11.8 characters, the median is 10. The distribution has a long right tail: a few hundred communes have names over 25 characters, and a handful top 35. The short end is nearly empty — one commune with 1 character, 13 with 2, 154 with 3.
