What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes. One of them is called Y. Another runs to 45 characters. And 3,891 begin with a saint.

2026-05-20 4 min read

France is a republic of 35,014 communes — villages, towns, arrondissements — each with an official name registered in the national code officiel géographique. Most names are unremarkable. Many are saints. Some are rivers. A handful are puzzles. And one of them, in the Somme département, is a single letter.

Y

The commune of Y — pronounced like the letter — sits in the Somme, between Amiens and Saint-Quentin. Population: 89. It holds a credible claim to the shortest place name in the world. The name has Norman roots, likely from the Latin word for water (aqua, contracted through centuries of dialect). France also has 13 two-character communes: Sy, By, Bû, Oô, Oz, Ri, Uz, Py, Ur, Gy, Eu, Ry, Us. That last one — Us — is in the Val-d'Oise, 30 km north-west of Paris.

A right-skewed country

Most French commune names cluster between 6 and 10 characters — the length of a saint's epithet, a river suffix, a short Norman noun. The distribution peaks at 7–8 characters and has a long right tail: some towns have accumulated centuries of fusions, hyphenations, and dedications until the name itself is a small sentence.

Longest commune name (45 characters)
Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson
Marne (51) · pop. 279 · formed by the merger of four villages in 1972

At 45 characters, Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson is France's longest commune name — the product of a 1972 administrative merger that stitched four villages together. It narrowly edges out Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 chars, Haute-Saône). French communes have been merging under the loi Marcellin and its successors since 1971; the long-hyphenated names are the visible residue of that policy.

The saint problem

3,891 communes — 11.1% of the total — begin with Saint- or Sainte-. That's before counting the 200-odd communes where "saint" appears mid-name, or those that use a local-language equivalent (San, Sen, Sant, Sent). The word saint appears across 4,090 commune names in total, making it the single most common word in France's administrative geography. More common than bois (217 communes), château (95), or mer (109).

35,014 communes, 32,764 names

France has 35,014 communes but only 32,764 distinct names. The 2,250 surplus are shared: 1,450 names belong to at least two different communes. The most duplicated name is Sainte-Colombe, which appears twelve times — in the Hautes-Alpes, Charente-Maritime, Doubs, Gironde, Ille-et-Vilaine, Landes, Lot, Manche, Rhône, Seine-Maritime, Seine-et-Marne, and Yonne. Twelve Sainte-Colombes, spread across France like a devotion that never quite settled in one place.

A country of rivers

1,991 communes — 5.7% — carry -sur- in their name: they sit on a river, and they named themselves accordingly. Chalon-sur-Saône. Argenteuil-sur-Armançon. Savigny-sur-Orge. The -lès- pattern (meaning "near") accounts for another 545: towns that defined themselves not by a waterway but by proximity to a more important neighbour. The -sous- pattern adds 257 more — communes literally "under" a hill or a château. French place names are a compressed topography.

The 35,014 names are a sediment layer. Norman roots, Latin saints, river names, feudal hyphenations, post-war mergers. Each tells you something about where France has been. Y tells you that even the shortest answer has a long history.

A note on the data. Commune names and populations come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which covers 35,014 communes in metropolitan France and overseas territories. Name analysis is based on the communeName field from this dataset, which follows the IGN Code officiel géographique conventions. Centroid coordinates are derived from commune polygon geometries in the same source. Preposition and article exclusions ("sur", "lès", "sous", "les", "des", "aux", "val") were applied manually for the word-frequency chart.
