What's in a name?

35,014 communes, 32,764 unique names — and one commune whose full official name is the letter Y.

2026-05-20 4 min read

France has 35,014 communes. They have been named — and renamed, merged, hyphenated, doubled, and occasionally named after a saint who exists in no other context — across twelve centuries of administration. The result is a list that includes the elegantly abbreviated, the improbably long, and the thoroughly duplicated.

35,014 Total communes
32,764 Unique names
1,450 Names shared by 2+ communes
4,195 Saint or Sainte names

That leaves 2,250 communes sharing a name with at least one other — 3,700 if you count the communes on the receiving end. Say you live in Saint-Martin. Eight other communes raise their hand. Say you live in Beaulieu. Nine more do. The postal system handles this via the département code; for everyone else, it's a small daily exercise in disambiguation.

The most popular name in France

Sainte-Colombe appears 12 times — twelve separate communes in twelve separate départements, each apparently home to a church dedicated to Saint Columba (or a memory of one). They run from the Hautes-Alpes to the Yonne, from Gironde to Seine-et-Marne. No two share a département. The pattern is less a coincidence than an artefact of medieval parish geography, where the patron saint of a local church frequently became the name of the surrounding settlement.

Saints dominate the top of the list — 16 of the top 20 most common names are Saint- or Sainte- forms. The exceptions: Beaulieu (10×), Le Pin (9×), Beaumont (8×), and Verrières (8×). Together the saint communes number 4,195, or just over 12% of all communes. France has more communes named Saint-Paul than it has communes in some entire départements.

Y

The shortest commune name in France is a single letter. Y — pronounced like the French letter ypsilon, rhyming roughly with ee-grek — sits in the Somme (département 80), population 89. It has a church, a mayor, an official INSEE code (80829), and an entry in every administrative database in the Republic. It is also, apparently, very difficult to search for online.

Y has company. There are 13 two-letter communes in France. They cluster slightly in the Pyrenees (Oô, Py, Ur, Uz) and the Norman and Burgundian north (By, Eu, Ry, Ri). Several are Basque or Occitan remnants where French orthography was simply applied to a pre-existing short root. Oô keeps its circumflex; Bû keeps its. The République has a policy on diacritics.

The other extreme

At the opposite end of the scale: Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson, in the Marne (département 51). Forty-five characters. It is a commune nouvelle — a merged administrative unit formed when three or four villages were consolidated, each keeping its historical name in the compound. The result is essentially a postal address that already contains several addresses.

The runner-up, Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 characters, Haute-Saône), follows the same logic: four former communes, one hyphenated administrative entity. Long names correlate strongly with departmental mergers — particularly those from the 2010s and 2020s when the loi NOTRe encouraged but did not require consolidation. The holdouts kept their names by attaching them to each other.

The geography of duplication follows a rough pattern: saint names spread liturgically, following the expansion of Frankish Christianity across different valleys and pays. Landscape names — Beaulieu (beautiful place), Beaumont (beautiful hill), Le Pin (the pine) — spread descriptively, applied independently wherever the landscape fit. Two separate medieval surveyors, standing on two separate hilltops, looked out at a view and wrote "Beaumont." Neither knew about the other.

A note on the data. Commune names and codes come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which draws its commune list and INSEE codes from the official Code Officiel Géographique (COG). The geometry used for centroid calculation is the 2025 simplified administrative boundary file from data.gouv.fr. Population figures are the INSEE 2021 base. Some very small communes and overseas territories show population 0 (data not published at commune level).
