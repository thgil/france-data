What's in a name?

35,014 French communes, 32,764 unique names, and one that's just the letter Y.

2026-06-02 4 min read

France has 35,014 officially recognised communes. Each one has a name. Most of the names are sensible — villages, saints, rivers, hills. But at the far end of the distribution, something unexpected: a commune with a name that's just one letter long.

Y

Population: 89. Département: Somme (80). INSEE code: 80829. The most concise address in France: Y, 80830.

The name probably traces to a Picard word for "water" or a corruption of an old toponym. Whatever its origin, it has survived every administrative reform since the Revolution. France has had the same one-letter commune for over 200 years.

Below Y on the length scale: thirteen communes with two-letter names — By (Doubs, pop. 86), Bû (Eure-et-Loir, pop. 2,022), Eu (Seine-Maritime, pop. 6,591), Gy (Haute-Saône, pop. 1,001), and eight others. All are old. All survived.

One in nine communes is a saint

France is officially a secular republic. Its commune map is not. 3,882 communes — 11.1% of the total — have names beginning with Saint- or Sainte-. Add the 147 places called La Chapelle-something and the 28 Notre-Dame compounds, and the ecclesiastical share grows further.

The Saint- prefix alone accounts for more communes than the entire département of Nord has residents divided by a thousand. This density reflects how France was organised for nearly a millennium: parishes came before communes, and when the Revolution reorganised everything in 1790, most places kept the name of their church's patron saint.

The problem of 12 Sainte-Colombes

Of the 35,014 commune names, only 32,764 are unique. That leaves 2,250 communes sharing a name with at least one other commune. The most-duplicated name in France is Sainte-Colombe — twelve different communes, spread across twelve different départements, from Haute-Garonne (31) to Seine-et-Marne (77).

Shortest names

Post a letter to "Sainte-Colombe, France" without specifying a department, and it could end up in any of twelve villages. The French postal system handles this with departmental codes — which is exactly why postcodes exist. The ambiguity is baked into the administrative DNA.

The 45-character commune

At the other end of the length scale: the longest commune name in France runs to 45 characters.

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson Marne (51) · pop. 491 · 45 characters

These tongue-twisting names are the legacy of France's commune merger law of 1972, which created communes nouvelles by fusing small villages. When two or three hamlets merged, their names were often concatenated with hyphens and ets. The result: administrative poetry. The runners-up are almost as long — Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 chars, Haute-Saône) and Saint-Martin-de-Bienfaite-la-Cressonnière (41 chars, Calvados).

The letter S dominates

More French communes begin with the letter S than any other — 5,598 of them, almost entirely because of Saint- and Sainte-. The runner-up is L (4,279 communes), powered by La, Le, Les, and La Chapelle-. The rarest starting letter is X, with just 21 communes. Starting with Y? 61 communes, led by the single-letter village that started this story.
