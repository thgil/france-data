What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes. One is called Y. Twelve are all called Sainte-Colombe. And one name is 45 characters long.

2026-05-24 4 min read

France has more communes than any other country in the European Union — 35,014 of them. That's more than Germany, Spain, Italy, and Poland combined. Naming that many places leaves marks. Some names are impossibly short. Some are absurdly long. And a surprising number of places simply share the same name entirely.

One letter versus forty-five

The shortest commune name in France is a single character: Y. It sits in the Somme, département 80, population 89. The name appears in Carolingian records — it is simply an old Picard word for water or a low-lying place. It has always been Y.

At the other end, Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson (45 characters) holds the record for length. It is not one place — it is three, fused by a 1972 merger. When French communes merged in the 1970s under the loi Marcellin, each constituent village fought to keep its name, producing hyphenated compounds that have never stopped growing. The second-longest, Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 characters), is another fusion. The pattern repeats.

A republic of saints

France abolished the monarchy in 1789 and the Church's legal privileges soon after. It did not, however, abolish the habit of naming communes after saints. Of the country's 35,014 communes, more than one in nine starts with Saint or Sainte:

3,898
communes whose name begins with Saint or Sainte — 11.1% of all communes in France.

The most common first word in a French commune name is Saint, which appears 3,596 times. La (1,054) and Le (758) are the runners-up. No republic has been able to replace this particular infrastructure.

Which name is most common?

1,450 distinct commune names are shared by two or more communes. In total, 3,700 communes share their name with at least one neighbour — somewhere across France. The post office has developed strategies for this.

The most common full commune name is Sainte-Colombe: twelve separate communes, in twelve different départements, from Normandy to the Pyrénées to Île-de-France.

35,014 names, and counting

Behind each of these names is a separate administrative unit with its own mayor, its own budget, its own road-maintenance contracts. France has been slowly reducing the count — the communes nouvelles programme merged thousands in the 2010s — but 35,014 is still the number. The names accumulate: saints, saints compounded with other saints, one-letter relics, forty-five-character monuments to municipal pride. The geography of naming is a secondary map of French history, and it's hiding in plain sight.

A note on the data. Commune names, codes, and geometries come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which is itself built on INSEE administrative boundaries. The 35,014 figure covers metropolitan France and overseas territories. Name analysis was performed on the communeName field; 14 features had null names and were excluded. The commune centroids are computed as polygon centroid approximations from the 1 km–simplified boundary file.
