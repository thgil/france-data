What's in a name?

35,014 communes, one single-letter village, 3,891 saints — a field guide to how France names its towns.

2026-06-06 4 min read

Somewhere in the Somme, between Amiens and Péronne, there is a commune called Y. Not an abbreviation. Not a truncation. Just the letter Y. Population: 89. It is the shortest place name in France — possibly the shortest official municipal name in Europe — and it has a functioning mairie, a war memorial, and a postcode.

France has 35,014 communes. Their names run from Y to a 45-character monster in the Marne. They carry river crossings, saints, royal estates, and the scar tissue of a thousand administrative mergers. Each one is a small fossil of whatever mattered when the place was first written down.

The shape of a name

The median commune name is 10 characters long. The mode is 7–8 — the comfortable territory of Beaumont, Pontoise, Breteuil. But the distribution has a long right tail: a hundred-odd communes have names over 30 characters, and a handful push past 40. These are merger names, compounds of two or three former villages stitched together when France rationalised its commune map in the 1970s.

The extremes

Thirteen communes have two-letter names — a roll call of very old, very compressed words: By, Bû, Eu, Gy, Oz, Oô, Py, Ri, Ry, Sy, Ur, Us, Uz. Most are pre-Latin in origin — Celtic or Gaulish roots compressed by time until only the consonant skeleton remained. Eu in Seine-Maritime is the largest at 6,591 people; Py in the Pyrénées-Orientales, 79 residents, is the smallest.

At the other extreme: Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson. Forty-five characters. A commune in the Marne (dept 51) formed in 1972 by the merger of three villages, with the bureaucratic ambition to name all three in full and still call it one place. It has 491 residents and a name that defeats most address forms.

The saint problem

3,891 communes — 11.1% of the total — begin with Saint or Sainte. That is not a religious observation: it reflects medieval parish organisation, where the local church and its patron defined the settlement's identity. The names survived the Revolution, which renamed dozens of saint-names to civic alternatives (Saint-Denis briefly became Franciade) but then mostly re-Sainted them within a decade.

The most common saint name is Sainte-Colombe — 12 communes across six different départements all claim it. Saint-Sauveur (11), Saint-Aubin (10), Saint-Marcel (9), Saint-Michel (9), Saint-Pierre (9): the top of the list reads like a medieval liturgical calendar. Postal services have learned to live with it.

The duplicate problem

35,014 communes, but only 32,764 unique names. That means 2,250 communes share their name with at least one other. If you send a letter to Beaulieu with no département code, it could land in any of ten different places. Beaumont has eight candidates. The INSEE commune code (the five-digit identifier that appears on every official document) exists precisely because the names do not uniquely identify anything.

Rivers, seas, and the art of the hyphen

1,991 communes stake their claim to a riverside position with a -sur- in their name. The sea leads with 99 communes à -sur-Mer; the Loire and the Seine each claim 70; the Marne accounts for 55 more. The hyphen itself is practically a French civic institution: 13,410 communes — 38% of the total — contain at least one. Some contain seven.

Most common first word

The commune name is a kind of compressed archaeology. Strip away the saints and you find river-crossings (Pont-, 63 communes), fortified hills (Mont-, 52), springs (Fontaine-, 58), new settlements (Villeneuve-, 70). The medieval village pattern is still perfectly legible in the directory, if you know what to look for. And at one end of the spectrum, blinking quietly in the Somme: Y.

A note on the data. Commune names, INSEE codes, and population figures come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which covers all 34,886–35,014 French communes with their INSEE administrative codes and 2021 census population. The communes-index.json built for this story (35,014 entries, ~3 MB) includes name, code, département, population, and centroid coordinates and is available as a reusable asset for future stories.
