What's in a French commune name?

35,014 commune names, one single-letter town, 3,898 saints, and the municipality whose official name takes 45 characters to type.

2026-05-14 5 min read

France has 35,014 communes. Each one has an official name — bestowed by history, geography, a local saint, or, in at least one remarkable case, a single letter of the alphabet. The DREES APL dataset, which we used for the medical deserts story, carries all of them: 35,014 communeNames, one per polygon. So we counted, sorted, and looked.

The shortest name in France

One commune's official name is a single letter.

Y

Y — commune in the Somme (département 80). Population: 89. INSEE code: 80829.

Y sits in Picardie, about 120 km north of Paris. It has been there since at least the Middle Ages — the name derives from an old Picard word for water. With 89 residents, it is one of France's smaller communes, but it holds an unchallenged record: the shortest official commune name in the country, and quite possibly in Europe.

One in nine communes is a saint

3,898 communes beginning with Saint or Sainte
11.1% of all French communes
1,450 distinct names shared by 2+ communes

One in nine French communes is named after a saint. Not just many — 3,898, amounting to 11.1% of the total. Add the saint-prefixed names like Villers-Saint-Frambourg or La Chapelle-Saint-Aubert and the count climbs higher. The word saint appears 4,074 times across all commune name components.

The most common full commune name is Sainte-Colombe — twelve separate communes in twelve départements, from the Hautes-Alpes to the Yonne. Saint-Sauveur exists eleven times. Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu each appear ten times.

The disambiguation problem

1,450 distinct names are shared by two or more communes — meaning 3,700 communes in total have a neighbour somewhere in France with the exact same name. France's postal system, administration, and GPS have all had to develop strategies for this. The usual solution is to append the département or a geographic qualifier: Beaulieu-sur-Dordogne vs Beaulieu-sur-Loire vs Beaulieu-sur-Mer. But the ten-fold duplicates just exist, unchanged, in their different corners of France.

A long tail of long names

The median commune name is 8 characters. Most names cluster between 5 and 12 characters. But the distribution has a very long right tail — driven partly by the French tradition of fused commune names created when two villages merged but retained both their old names joined by -et-. There are 358 such fusion names in the data.

The longest commune name in France is Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson — 45 characters, département Marne.

It was created by merging three smaller communes in 1972. The full name preserves all three original villages: Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont, Saint-Genest, and Isson. The second-longest (43 characters) is Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur in Haute-Saône — another fusion, another hyphen-chain, another administrative compromise that outlasted everyone involved.

The full shape of France, in words

2,220 communes begin with an article — Le, La, Les, L'. Another 105 start with Villers, a Germanic root meaning "settlement," left across northern France from the Frankish expansion. 63 start with Val, 58 with Fontaine, 52 with Mont.

The names are a sediment layer of history: Roman roads, monastic dedications, topographic accidents, medieval mergers, Revolutionary renaming, modern administrative fusions. And somewhere in the Somme, 89 people live in a place called Y, and have done for a thousand years.

A note on the data. Commune names come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which covers 35,014 French communes including overseas territories. A small number of communes appear more than once in the APL data when they were split across administrative boundaries; these are counted separately. The communes-index.json file bundled with this story (35,014 entries, ~3 MB) is a lightweight name + centroid extract from the same source, reusable by future stories without requiring the full 10 MB GeoJSON.
