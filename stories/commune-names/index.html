35,014 communes, 32,764 names

France has more communes than any other country in the EU — and almost 3,000 of them share a name with somewhere else. One is called Y.

2026-05-29 4 min read

France has 35,014 communes — the most of any EU member state by a wide margin. It's a legacy of the Revolution, which in 1789 turned every parish in the kingdom into a self-governing unit and then left most of them alone for the next 230 years. The result is a country with more municipalities than the rest of the EU combined.

But the real surprise is the names. 35,014 communes, yet only 32,764 distinct names. Nearly 2,300 name values are shared by more than one place. One in ten French communes — 3,700 of them — shares its name with at least one other commune somewhere in the country.

Sainte-Colombe wins

The most duplicated commune name in France is Sainte-Colombe, carried by 12 different communes across 12 departments — from Hautes-Alpes to Yonne. Saint-Sauveur is close behind at 11, then Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu at 10 each. The pattern is unmistakeable: France's most shared names are almost all saints' names, a direct echo of the parish geography that created the commune system.

3,898 communes — 11.1% of the total — start with Saint or Sainte. One in nine French communes is named after a saint.

Add Le, La, and Les (2,140 communes) and you've accounted for nearly 18% of France's communes with just four words. The country's administrative geography is, in a very literal sense, a catalogue of medieval Catholicism and feudal landscape description.

One letter to forty-five

At one extreme: Y. A commune of 89 people in the Somme. One letter. No disambiguation needed — there is only one Y in France. It has a mayor, a church, and a perfectly valid INSEE code (80829).

At the other extreme: Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson. Forty-five characters. This village of roughly 850 people in the Marne achieved its extraordinary name through successive mergers in the 19th century — the French administrative equivalent of a hyphenated double-barrelled name applied three times running.

      The geography beneath the names

      The 35,014 communes range from Paris 1er Arrondissement (17,000 residents) to dozens of villages with fewer than 30 people. The smallest are concentrated in the same interior band that shows up as a medical desert and a bar desert — the diagonale du vide, running from the Meuse to the Pyrénées. These tiny communes often carry names that have existed, essentially unchanged, for a thousand years.

      The duplicate names aren't random — they follow religious geography. A medieval bishop's diocese would contain multiple villages named after the same patron saint: all of them now independent communes, all of them waiting for their post code to distinguish them from each other. Sainte-Colombe (Rhône) and Sainte-Colombe (Seine-Maritime) have never met. They share only a name, a saint, and a country.

      A note on the data. Commune names come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset joined to Etalab's contours administratifs — 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Name analysis (duplicates, lengths, saint prefixes) computed from the communes-index.json file built for this story. Centroids are polygon averages, not official cadastral centroids.
