What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes — and one of them is called Y. A tour of the country's most duplicated, shortest, and longest place names.

2026-05-28 4 min read
Y
Commune in Somme (dept 80). Population: 89. The shortest official place name in France — and one of the shortest in the world.

Y exists. It is a real village in the Somme with 89 residents, a church, and a post office that technically doesn't need to spell out the commune name. The address just says Y. France's administrative code for it is 80829. That code, issued by INSEE, is nine characters longer than the place itself.

Y is the extreme case, but it's not alone. Thirteen other communes have two-letter names — ancient roots in Frankish, Occitan, Basque, and Catalan that survived every standardisation campaign Paris could throw at them:

A country of long names

The average French commune name runs to 11.8 characters. That's long — longer than most European equivalents — because France hyphenates aggressively. A village and its neighbouring hamlet merge; both names survive, joined by a dash. Names accumulate prefixes: Saint-, La Chapelle-, Le Mesnil-. Repeat for a thousand years and you end up with a country where "11 characters" is average and the tail of long names stretches far to the right.

The 45-character champion

At the far end of that tail sits:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson
Dept 51 (Marne) · Population 491 · 45 characters · INSEE code 51513

The name is a commune nouvelle record — the result of merging Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont with Saint-Genest-et-Isson. French law since 1971 has encouraged mergers but doesn't require name simplification, so both identities survive, hyphenated. The result is a name that won't fit on most printed forms without overflowing the field. Second place goes to Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 characters, Haute-Saône), which managed to pack four former villages into one name.

Christianity ran the naming desk for a thousand years. The Saint- prefix alone accounts for 3,587 communes; Sainte- adds another 295. Below them, the next most popular starting words reveal a land of chapels (La Chapelle-, 152 communes), hills (Villers-, 105), new towns (Villeneuve-, 70), and bridges (Pont-, 62).

The most common name: Sainte-Colombe

3,700 French communes share their name with at least one other commune. The worst offender is Sainte-Colombe — twelve different villages, spread across twelve departments from Gironde (33) to Yonne (89), all honouring the same third-century martyr. If you tell someone you're from Sainte-Colombe, you'll need to specify the département. The post office figured this out centuries ago; the INSEE code — a 5-digit number unique to each commune — was invented precisely to solve this problem.

Why INSEE codes exist

Of France's 35,014 communes, 32,764 have unique names — a surprisingly high share given the pressure of shared saints and prefixes. The remaining 3,700 collide: two Beaulieus in Isère alone, nine different Saint-Marcels, ten Saint-Aubins across ten departments. The five-digit INSEE commune code, introduced systematically in the twentieth century, is the solution: two digits for the département, three for the commune within it. It turns an ambiguous name into an unambiguous identifier.

That code is what drives every dataset on this site. The APL medical-desert scores, the pharmacy counts, the bar locations — all of them join on INSEE codes rather than names, because Saint-Aubin is a name that belongs to ten places at once. The names are cultural; the numbers are administrative. France keeps both, carefully, and that is the quiet genius of its open data.

A note on the data. Commune names, codes, populations, and département assignments come from the medical-deserts story's source file: a 10 MB GeoJSON of 35,014 communes built from DREES APL 2023 scores joined to Etalab's 2025 administrative boundary contours. Centroids were computed as the average of polygon vertices (suitable for labelling; not geodetically rigorous). Name-length and frequency analysis is performed client-side at load time from the communes-index.json file (3.4 MB).
