A country called Sainte-Colombe

35,014 commune names, from a single letter to 45 characters — and 12 different places all called the same thing.

2026-05-07 5 min read

Since the French Revolution, every patch of inhabited land in France has carried a legal name registered with the state. Today there are 35,014 of those names. Some are shared by a dozen different places. One is a single letter.

Starting with Saint-

One letter

The shortest commune name in France is Y. Just that — one letter, one vowel, nothing else. It sits in the Somme, département 80, population 89. Mail addressed to Y, 80190, France is legitimate correspondence. The municipality exists, votes in elections, elects a mayor, and appears on road signs. It has its own postcode.

Y is not an abbreviation for anything. It is the full name, traceable to a Latin root meaning "water." The village sits near a small river. The name stuck for a thousand years and saw no reason to grow.

The two-letter club

Thirteen communes manage with just two characters. Several carry diacritical marks that the postal system occasionally ignores — Bû and Oô both have to defend their circumflex and trema against standardisation. Eu, in Seine-Maritime (population 6,591), is large enough to appear in a French Scrabble dictionary as a legal played word.

The Saint epidemic

When France converted to Christianity, it named everything after its saints. When the Revolution tried to rename the saint-towns, most towns ignored the order and reverted within a decade. The result: 3,882 communes — 11.1% of all French communes — still start with Saint- or Sainte-. It is the dominant first element of any French commune name, and nothing else comes close.

The Sainte-Colombe problem

The most duplicated commune name in France is Sainte-Colombe. Twelve separate communes share it exactly — spread across twelve different départements, from the Charente-Maritime to the Yonne, from the Landes to the Seine-et-Marne. If you write a letter to Sainte-Colombe, France without specifying a département, you have addressed it to twelve possible recipients.

Shortest commune names

Saint-Sauveur follows with 11 copies; Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu each appear 10 times. The duplication is not administrative chaos — each commune has a unique INSEE code, a unique postcode, its own mayor. The names just happen to converge on the same saints and the same landscapes, independently, across centuries.

How long is a commune name?

The average French commune name is 11.8 characters. Most names cluster between 6 and 16 characters — long enough to be distinctive, short enough to fit on a sign. But the distribution has a long right tail.

The hyphenated titans

At 45 characters, Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson holds the record. It is not a whim. It is the result of post-Revolution administrative mergers that fused multiple hamlets into a single commune — and the name had to contain all of them. Each hyphen is a historical negotiation. Each segment is a former hamlet that refused to be forgotten.

Six communes exceed 40 characters. Seventy-nine exceed 30. They are all in the same category: merge-products, administrative consolidations from the 19th and 20th centuries where the solution was always to concatenate rather than choose.

Fossil record

France's 35,014 commune names are not arbitrary. They are a compressed history of the country's settlement patterns — saints from the medieval church, topographic features (mont, val, pont, fontaine), remnants of Breton, Occitan, Alsatian, and Basque place-name systems, and the administrative archaeology of a thousand years of mergers, splits, and state-building.

The names are stable in a way that French politics never has been. Borders changed, kings fell, republics rose and fell — but Y kept its one letter, Sainte-Colombe stayed twelve, and the hyphens of Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson held their ground.

A note on the data. Commune names and populations come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which covers 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Population figures are from the INSEE 2021 census base. Name analysis is computed from the communeName field in the source GeoJSON.
