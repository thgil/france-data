What's in a name?

35,014 French communes. One is called Y. Over 3,800 begin with Saint. Here is the full picture of French place names, by the numbers.

2026-06-01 5 min read

Somewhere in the Somme, between Rouen and Amiens, there is a commune called Y. Not "Why" — Y. One letter. 89 residents. A mairie. A church. A bus stop. The name has been recorded that way since at least the twelfth century, from the old Picard word for water. It is the shortest official place name in France, and almost certainly the shortest in Europe.

Y France's shortest commune name. Population: 89. Département: Somme (80).

France counts 35,014 communes — more than any other country in the EU. Each carries a name, some ancient, some bureaucratic, some accidental. Together they form a taxonomy of the country's history: its saints, its rivers, its forests, its feudal estates, its mergers, and its sheer love of the hyphen.

The two-letter club

Thirteen communes share the distinction of two-letter names. They scatter across the country with no obvious pattern — from the Ardennes to the Pyrénées-Orientales, from Normandy to Haute-Saône. Eu (Seine-Maritime, 6,591 residents) is the most populous. Uz (Hautes-Pyrénées, 36 residents) is the most isolated. Gy (Haute-Saône, 1,001) is home to a famous Champagne house.

The Saint phenomenon

The single biggest driver of commune names in France is the Catholic Church — specifically its calendar of saints. 3,898 communes, 11.1% of the total, start with Saint or Sainte. That is why S is by far the most common starting letter, with nearly 5,600 entries — almost triple the second-place L (4,279, dominated by Le, La, and Les).

The hyphen is almost as ubiquitous as the saint. 13,410 communes — 38.3% of all French place names — contain at least one hyphen. France's administrative history of merging villages, appending river names, and distinguishing identically-named communes through compound names has produced a geography that is, on paper, exhausting.

Sainte-Colombe, everywhere

Most commune names are unique: 89.4% of names appear exactly once. But 2,250 distinct names are shared by two or more communes. The winner is Sainte-Colombe, which appears in 12 different départements. Behind it come Saint-Sauveur (11), Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu (10 each). There are nine communes called Saint-Pierre and nine called Saint-Marcel.

The long end of the distribution

At the opposite extreme from Y sits Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson. Forty-five characters, including four hyphens, two saints, and the traces of at least three administrative mergers. It is a commune in the Marne with 491 residents, a mairie in a small stone building, and a name that does not fit on most forms. The top five longest names share a common structure: they are all fusions, carrying the ghosts of the villages that merged into them.

The shape of French place names

The average commune name is 11.8 characters long. The distribution peaks at 7–9 characters — names like Ambleny, Beaumont, or Castres — then tapers off with a long right tail of compounded, hyphenated, fused toponyms stretching past 30 characters. The 1-letter commune stands alone at the far left; the 45-character behemoth stands alone at the far right.

The names are a document. They record which saints were popular in which century, which rivers defined which territories, which villages were merged in which prefectural reorganisation, and which local bureaucrats decided to split a disambiguation with a hyphen rather than a comma. Y didn't choose its name. Neither did Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson. The country accumulated both, and kept them.

A note on the data. Commune names and population figures come from the DREES APL 2023 commune-level dataset, which covers 35,014 communes across metropolitan France and overseas territories. A small number of communes in the APL dataset reflect administrative geography as of 2021–2023, after several post-2016 fusion waves; the total count is slightly lower than the 35,400+ codes in the 2025 COG because fused communes inherit a single entry. Name lengths and frequencies are computed from the communeName field in the APL GeoJSON.
