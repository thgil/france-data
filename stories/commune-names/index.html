What's in a name?

35,014 communes, 32,764 unique names, and one that is just a single letter.

2026-05-17 5 min read

Y is a commune in the Somme. Population 89. It sits between Roye and Saint-Quentin, surrounded by fields, on a plain that has seen more than its share of history. Its name comes from a Norman-Picard root — y or ie, meaning a wet place, a location near water. The word is older than the kingdom of France. The commune is one letter long.

It is the shortest commune name in France. It is also, by most counts, the shortest official place name in the world.

Y is not alone. France has 13 more communes whose names are just two letters — most of them in regions where old Norman, Breton, and Occitan roots compressed into monosyllables that never expanded again. Eu (Seine-Maritime, 6,591 residents) is probably the most visited of the group. Oô is in the Hautes-Pyrénées. Uz is in the same range, with 36 residents. Sy, in the Ardennes, has 54.

The saint epidemic

Pick a French commune at random. There is roughly a one-in-nine chance its name starts with Saint- or Sainte-. That is not hyperbole: 3,882 communes — 11.1% of the total — begin with one of those two prefixes. Expand the search to any commune with "saint" anywhere in its name and the count reaches 4,459.

This is not piety; it is parish geography. Medieval France was administered through church parishes, each named for its patron saint. When the Revolution created the commune system in 1789, it used the existing parish network as its skeleton. Most communes kept their names. The saints stayed.

Ten Saint-Aubins, twelve Sainte-Colombes

France has 35,014 communes. It has only 32,764 distinct names. The 2,250-commune gap is the duplicate problem: communes that share an identical name with at least one other, scattered across different départements. This is why every French address includes the département — without it, a letter to "Saint-Aubin" has a one-in-ten chance of going to the wrong place. There are ten of them.

The single most duplicated name is Sainte-Colombe, appearing in twelve separate communes across ten différent départements, from the Aisne in the north to the Var in the south. Saint-Sauveur follows at eleven. Below are the twenty most duplicated names in France.

The Loi Marcellin names

At the opposite extreme from Y and Sy is a commune in the Marne whose name is 45 characters long.

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson Marne (51) · population 491 · 45 characters

The name reads like a marriage contract — because it essentially is one. In 1972, France passed the Loi Marcellin, which encouraged small communes to merge by granting them the option to hyphenate both names together rather than drop one. The incentive structure produced some of the longest municipal names on the planet. The second-longest, at 43 characters, is Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur in Haute-Saône (pop. 923). Third: Saint-Martin-de-Bienfaite-la-Cressonnière in Calvados (pop. 499). All three contain at least one saint.

The shape of a French commune name

The typical French commune name is 7 to 9 characters long — the natural width of a simple place-word: Béziers, Rodez, Chartres, Quimper. That window accounts for 10,390 communes, almost 30% of the total. The distribution has a long right tail: 187 communes have names exceeding 28 characters, all of them merger products from the 1970s onward. The left tail contains just one commune. You already know which one.

France has been naming places since before it was France. Some of those names are Gaulish, some Latin, some Norse, some simply the name of a family that owned the land. The 1789 commune law crystallised them into official administrative units. The 1972 merger law added hyphens. The linguist Dauzat counted over 35,000 distinct root elements across French commune names. The database counts 32,764 unique names across 35,014 communes. The difference, 2,250, is the number of places that had to share.

A note on the data. Commune names, codes, populations, and geometries come from the contours administratifs des communes 2025, published by data.gouv.fr / Etalab, joined to the DREES 2023 APL dataset for population figures. All 35,014 communes in metropolitan France and overseas territories are included. Name analysis performed on the communeName field of the joined dataset.
