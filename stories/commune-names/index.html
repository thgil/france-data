What's in a name?

35,014 commune names, from a single letter to 45. Here's what France calls itself.

2026-05-16 4 min read

France has a commune called Y. Not "Wy" or "Ye" — just Y. One character. Population 89. It sits in the Somme, roughly halfway between Amiens and Saint-Quentin, and has been called Y since at least the 13th century. Linguists think the name derives from an old Picard word for water. The postal service, presumably, manages.

Y

Commune de Y, département 80 (Somme). Population: 89. The shortest commune name in France — one letter, established since the Middle Ages. APL score 2.78: not a medical desert, which at least is something.

Y is the extreme, but it's not alone. France has 13 communes with two-letter names — a scattered set of place names that reads like someone dropped a handful of Scrabble tiles on a map: Sy, By, Bû, Oô, Oz, Ri, Uz, Py, Ur, Gy, Eu, Ry, Us. Most are tiny — Uz in Hautes-Pyrénées has 36 residents. Eu in Seine-Maritime (pop. 6,591) is the largest, an old royal town where French kings once signed treaties.

The bell curve of naming

Across all 35,014 communes, the average name is 11.8 characters long — but that average is pulled rightward by the long-tailed mountain towns and merged villages. The median is 10 characters. The distribution peaks sharply at 7–8 characters: Amiens, Romorantin, Chartres, ordinary French place names that fit on a sign without squinting. Then it spreads out in a long right tail, toward the bureaucratic epic poetry of France's merged communes.

Communes nouvelles and the long tail

The longest commune name in France stretches to 45 characters: Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson, in the Marne. It was born in 2016 when the French state encouraged rural commune mergers through the communes nouvelles programme — and the three villages involved simply concatenated their names. The runner-up is Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur in Haute-Saône (43 characters). These names are technically correct and administratively official. GPS devices reportedly cope poorly.

The longest commune names

    One nation, under Saint

    If France has a naming obsession, it is saints. Of the 35,014 communes, 3,891 begin with "Saint-" or "Sainte-" — one in nine. The word "saint" or "sainte" appears somewhere in 4,437 commune names, 12.7% of the total. The most common content word in French commune names, by a wide margin, is saint. The second is sur. France is a country of sacred rivers.

    The duplicate problem

    France's naming traditions mean that 10.6% of communes share their exact name with at least one other commune. The most duplicated name is Sainte-Colombe — 12 communes, spread across 12 different départements, from Ardèche (07) to Yonne (89) to Seine-et-Marne (77). There are 11 communes called Saint-Sauveur, 10 called Saint-Aubin, 10 called Beaulieu. When you write a letter to Sainte-Colombe, you really do need the département.

    89.4% of communes do have a unique name — which means 31,314 place names appear exactly once. But uniqueness is a harder standard than it sounds: Saint-Martin-de-la-Lieue (Calvados) is technically unique, even though there are 255 communes named after Saint Martin in some form. France distinguishes its saints; it just shares them.

    A note on the data. Commune names and basic demographic data come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, joined to the Etalab administrative boundary GeoJSON (1 km simplification). The dataset covers 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Name analysis, length statistics, and duplicate counts were computed from that dataset. The resulting communes-index.json (35,014 entries with code, name, département, population, and centroid coordinates) is published alongside this story for reuse.
