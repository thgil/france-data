What's in a commune name?

35,014 commune names. One is a single letter. One in nine starts with "Saint." And twelve villages share the exact same name.

2026-06-02 4 min read

In the Somme, 130 km north of Paris, there is a commune called Y. One letter. No hyphen, no article, no saint — just a single capital letter, the shortest official place name in France. It has around 100 residents and a mairie like everywhere else.

France has 35,014 communes, and their names are a living document of a thousand years of settlement, religion, geography, and administrative merger. The shortest name is one character. The longest runs to 45. Between them, a country names itself.

These are not typos. Oô (Haute-Garonne) has a circumflex that makes it look like a face. Eu (Seine-Maritime) is a royal château town with 8,000 residents. Gy (Haute-Saône) makes excellent Burgundy-adjacent wine. Short does not mean small.

The saints take over

3,882 communes — 11.1% of France — start with Saint or Sainte. The saint is so ubiquitous in French place names that it functions less like a religious title and more like a definite article. The most common family: names beginning Saint-Martin, with 203 variants — Saint-Martin-des-Champs, Saint-Martin-du-Mont, Saint-Martin-Rivière, and 200 more.

Ardèche leads in proportion: 29% of its communes carry a saint's name. Dordogne has the most in absolute terms — 130 out of 503. These are the deep rural heartlands of medieval Christendom, still legible in the gazetteer.

The most common name in France

Ask which commune name appears most often and the answer is Sainte-Colombe. Twelve communes across twelve different départements share this exact name — from Hautes-Alpes (05) to Yonne (89). Each is presumably named for Colombe of Sens, a third-century martyr, or one of several other early saints named Columba. None of them are near each other.

The runner-up is Saint-Sauveur with 11 occurrences; Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu each appear 10 times. There are 1,450 names shared by two or more communes, covering 3,700 communes in total — meaning roughly 1 in 10 communes shares its exact name with at least one other.

How long is a commune name?

The average commune name is 11.8 characters. Most cluster between 6 and 11 — the sweet spot of a single word or a simple two-element hyphenate. But the distribution has a long right tail, pulled by compound names that accumulated over centuries and mergers.

Longest — 45 characters

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson

A commune in the Marne (51), formed from the merger of three villages in 1972. All three kept their names, joined with hyphens.

The longest name is a masterclass in French administrative pragmatism: when villages merge, nobody wants to give up their name. Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson carries three former communes inside one hyphenated string. There are 167 communes with four or more hyphens — each one a small archive of a merger negotiation.

89% of commune names are unique — they appear exactly once in the national directory. The remaining 11% are a record of parallel settlement: the same landscape features, the same patron saints, the same Latin and Germanic roots, independently naming the same kinds of places across a very old country.

A note on the data. Commune names and codes come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset (accessed via the medical-deserts story pipeline), which draws on INSEE's official commune directory. Coverage is 35,014 communes including metropolitan France and overseas territories. Name lengths include all characters: letters, hyphens, apostrophes, and spaces.
