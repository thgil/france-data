What's in a name

One commune is called Y. Twelve are called Sainte-Colombe. And the longest name takes a full breath to say.

2026-06-05 4 min read

France has 35,014 communes. Each one has a name — and those names, taken together, are a compressed history of the country: its Christianization, its administrative revolutions, its mergers, its etymology from Latin and Frankish and Gaulish. The shortest is one letter long. The most duplicated appears in twelve separate départements. The longest requires a full breath to say. None of these facts appears on any tourist map.

A commune called Y

In the Somme, département 80, there is a commune with 89 residents, a medieval church, a mairie, and the most concise postal address in France: Y. That is its full official name — one letter, capital Y, derived from the Old Picard word for water. Situated near a marshy tributary of the Somme, the name predates the Revolution by several centuries and survived every administrative reform since.

Y is not alone. Thirteen communes have two-letter names. They cluster in Normandy, the Pyrenees, and the Franche-Comté — all regions with deep pre-French linguistic layers: Frankish, Occitan, Basque, and Gaulish.

After the two-letter tier, the three-letter names number 154. The pyramid quickly widens: 720 four-letter communes, nearly 2,000 with five. The average commune name is 11.8 characters long; the median, 10.

The saint army

The single most distinctive feature of French commune names is saints. One in 8 communes — 4,437 of 35,014, or 12.7% — contains Saint or Sainte somewhere in its name. More than 3,880 of those start with Saint- or Sainte- as the first word. No other starting word comes close: the next most common is Villers, which starts just 105 communes.

This is a direct legacy of post-Carolingian Christianization. When two communes shared a geographical root — both on a river, both on a hill, both in a forest — the easiest way to distinguish them was to dedicate each to a different saint. The 1790 Revolution attempted to laicize commune names; it succeeded almost nowhere. The saints stayed.

35,014 communes, 32,764 names

Despite 35,014 communes, France only has 32,764 distinct names. The 2,250-name shortfall means that roughly one commune in sixteen shares its exact name with at least one other — in a different département, sometimes on the other side of the country.

The most duplicated name in France is Sainte-Colombe, which appears 12 times in 12 different départements. From the English Channel coast to the edge of the Pyrenees, from Brittany to Burgundy — twelve villages, one name, dedicated to Saint Columba of Sens, a third-century martyr.

The name you cannot abbreviate

At the other end of the scale, the longest commune name in France belongs to a commune nouvelle in the Marne (département 51) — a merger of three historic villages that simply concatenated all their names rather than choose one:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson 45 characters · Marne (51) · commune nouvelle

The runner-up, Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 characters, Haute-Saône), follows the same logic: three villages merged after 2010, three names stapled together. This is the visible trace of France's communes nouvelles programme, which has steadily merged small communes since 2015. When villages merge, the names often merge too — a kind of palimpsest in reverse.

Geography compressed into text

France's 35,014 commune names are a geological record of settlement. They tell you where people farmed along rivers (beau-lieu, beautiful place), where they worshipped (Saint- everything), and where medieval administrators lost patience with disambiguation (Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson). The data behind this story — 35,014 names, their centroid coordinates, their populations — is 3 MB of JSON. It fits in a browser tab. The history it encodes took a thousand years to accumulate.

A note on the data. Commune names and boundaries come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset joined to administrative contours (Etalab, 2025 vintage), the same source used in Story 2 (medical deserts). The dataset covers 35,014 communes — all metropolitan departments plus overseas territories. Population figures are INSEE 2021 estimates. Centroid coordinates are derived from polygon bounding boxes.
