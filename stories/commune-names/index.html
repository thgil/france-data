What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes. One is called Y. Twelve are called Sainte-Colombe. And the longest runs to 45 characters.

2026-06-07 5 min read

Every commune in France has a name. Some are stately and ancient — Versailles, Bordeaux, Strasbourg. Some are descriptive: Fontaine-l'Évêque, Villeneuve-lès-Avignon, Montagne-de-la-Roque. And one is just: Y.

Y (pronounced "ee" in French) sits in the Somme, département 80, population 89. It has been a commune since the French Revolution reorganised the country into its current grid of roughly 35,000 municipalities. It has a church. It has a mayor. It has the shortest possible place name in France — and almost certainly in Europe.

The shortest commune name in France. One letter, Somme (80), population 89.

Y is not alone in the single-syllable bracket. France has 13 two-letter communes, mostly found in the north and the Pyrénées: By, Eu, Gy, Oz, Ri, Ry, Us. These aren't abbreviations or administrative codes — they're the actual names of places where people live and vote and have the post arrive.

Twelve Sainte-Colombes

At the other extreme of the naming distribution, some names appear so often that they've lost all geographic specificity. Sainte-Colombe is the most common commune name in France: there are twelve of them, spread across nine différent départements. If you send a letter to "Sainte-Colombe, France" without a postcode, you have put the postal service in a genuinely difficult position.

Saint-Sauveur appears eleven times. Saint-Aubin ten. Beaulieu ten. Saint-Marcel, Saint-Michel, Saint-Pierre, and Sainte-Marie each appear nine times. In all, 1,450 different names are shared by more than one commune — meaning 3,700 French municipalities share their name with at least one other place in the country.

One in nine communes is a saint

France named a very large number of its communes after Christian saints. Precisely 3,882 communes — 11.1% of the total — begin with Saint- or Sainte-. That's one in nine. The masculine form (Saint-) dominates at 3,587 communes versus 295 for the feminine Sainte-.

The most popular saints? Colombe, Sauveur, Aubin, Marcel, Michel. Saint-Pierre is something of a runner-up at nine, which still feels low given that "Saint Peter" has been a default naming choice for churches since at least the 6th century. The real star of the data is Colombe — an obscure Gaulish martyr whose name nonetheless adorned twelve French villages, scattered from Haute-Savoie to Aveyron.

The median commune name is 10 characters long. The mode sits at 7–8. Most names are short: a single common noun (Autun, Crépy, Evreux), a suffix attached to a geographic feature (Pontarlier, Fontainebleau). But a long tail of merged and hyphenated names pulls the distribution rightward, all the way to 45 characters.

45 characters, population 491

The longest commune name in France belongs to a village in the Marne:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson 45 characters · Marne (51) · population 491 · formed by merger of three villages in 2016

The name is a historical document. Three separate villages — Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont, Saint-Genest, and Isson — were merged into a single commune under France's 2010 municipal reform law. None of them wanted to be absorbed into the others' identity, so the new commune kept all three names, joined by hyphens, producing a 45-character label that fits awkwardly on any form that asks for "commune de naissance."

The runner-up is Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur in Haute-Saône — 43 characters, another merger. Third place, at 41 characters, goes to Saint-Martin-de-Bienfaite-la-Cressonnière in Calvados. Notice a pattern: almost every entry on the "longest names" list is a post-2010 merger that tried to preserve all its old parts.

The grid holds the names

France's commune grid — the roughly 35,000 municipalities established after 1789 — is one of the most granular administrative structures in the world. Most countries have far fewer, far larger units. France has more communes than the rest of the EU combined. The result is an enormous list of names, most of them small, most of them ancient, many of them repeated.

Y has 89 residents and a name with one letter. Sainte-Colombe happens twelve times. Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson has 491 residents and a name that won't fit on a single line of a standard address label. The grid is exact, exhaustive, and occasionally slightly absurd. Très français.

A note on the data. Commune names come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset joined to INSEE commune boundaries (2025 vintage), used in Story 2 (medical deserts). The dataset covers 35,014 communes in metropolitan France and overseas territories. Name deduplication and length analysis were computed from the communeName field in that GeoJSON. Centroid coordinates are polygon-averaged. The communes-index.json file (3.1 MB) stores names, centroids, département codes, and populations for all 35,014 communes.
