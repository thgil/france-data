What's in a name?

France has 35,000 communes, 32,000 different names, one single-letter town, and twelve places called Sainte-Colombe.

2026-06-02 4 min read

France has 35,014 communes. Each one has an official name registered with the INSEE code system. Some of those names run to forty-five characters. One of them is a single letter.

Y Commune in the Somme, département 80. Population: 89.

The village of Y sits in the Somme valley, east of Amiens, 89 residents and one of the shortest place names on Earth. It's been Y since at least the 13th century — the name likely derives from water (eau, or the Old Picard i, meaning a stretch of river), not from any alphabetic preference. The post office has managed.

Below Y, there are thirteen two-letter communes: By (Doubs), Bû (Eure-et-Loir), Eu (Seine-Maritime), Gy (Haute-Saône), Oz (Isère), Oô (Haute-Garonne), Py (Pyrénées-Orientales), Ri (Orne), Ry (Seine-Maritime), Sy (Ardennes), Ur (Pyrénées-Orientales), Us (Val-d'Oise), and Uz (Hautes-Pyrénées). The Pyrénées produce two of them — Basque and Catalan toponymy runs to brisk, consonant-spare syllables.

One in nine communes is a saint

France is a secular republic, but its commune map is a liturgical calendar. 3,898 of 35,014 communes — 11.1% — begin with Saint or Sainte. That's a lot of patron saints. They range from Saint-Acheul (an Iron Age archaeological site in Somme that canonized its own prehistoric layer) to Saint-Zwern — a Breton saint so obscure that even hagiographic dictionaries hedge. The saints are distributed uniformly enough that whatever département you're in, statistically one in nine neighbours lives in a saint's commune.

The most popular saint? Sainte Colombe — martyr, 3rd century, Sens. She appears in the commune registry twelve times, scattered across eleven départements from the Hautes-Alpes to Seine-et-Marne. Sainte-Sauveur runs second (11 communes), then Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu (10 each). Beaulieu isn't a saint — it just means beautiful place — but France has ten of those too, apparently all convinced theirs is the real one.

The twelve Sainte-Colombes

When twelve different communes share one name, the differentiation happens at the département code level — officially they're Sainte-Colombe (05), Sainte-Colombe (17) and so on. In practice, locals just say their commune name and assume you know which one they mean. The twelve range from a hamlet of 59 people in the Hautes-Alpes to a village of 1,953 near Lyon.

Other highly duplicated names

Seven hyphens, forty-five characters

At the other end of the length scale, France has a tradition of merged communes that never bothered to shorten their combined name. The record holder is in the Marne:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson — Marne, département 51. Population 491. Name length: 45 characters, 7 hyphens.

It got that way through a 19th-century merger of several villages, each insisting on keeping its own toponym. The result is a name that exceeds most tweet character budgets for a single word. Second place goes to Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur in Haute-Saône (43 characters), which sounds less like a place and more like a law firm. Thirteen communes have names of 40 characters or more.

Hyphenated names are a majority feature: 13,410 communes — 38.3% — use at least one hyphen. Many are simple saint-plus-preposition constructions (Saint-Martin-de-Ré), while others record the entire administrative inheritance of the Ancien Régime in a single compound noun.

32,764 unique answers

Of 35,014 commune names, 32,764 are unique — 93.6%. The 2,250 that are duplicates are almost entirely Saints and generic landscape descriptions (Le Pin, Beaumont, Les Bruyères). Every duplicated name is a small disambiguation problem that French address systems solve with the département code — a workaround that has been in place since the Revolution established the current administrative structure in 1790.

The geography of naming is a record of Christianisation, Romanisation, Norse and Frankish settlement, Breton and Basque language survival, and the relentless tendency of merged villages to hyphenate rather than choose. Somewhere in that record is Y, population 89, holding down one end of the distribution since the thirteenth century.

A note on the data. Commune names and their INSEE codes come from the APL géolocalisation file published by DREES, which covers 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Name counts are exact string matches — accent-sensitive. The analysis covers the 2023–2025 commune boundary vintage. A small number of rows carry arrondissement-level names (Marseille, Lyon, Paris) rather than commune names; these are included in the count but do not affect the key findings.
