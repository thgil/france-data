A country named after saints

35,014 communes. One is called Y. 3,898 start with Saint. The most common name belongs to 12 different places.

2026-05-14 4 min read

France has 35,014 communes — more than any other EU country. Each has an official name registered with the INSEE code system. Most names are unremarkable: a handful of syllables, a saint, a river, a direction. But spend a few minutes with the full list and the edges get strange. There is exactly one commune in France whose entire name is a single letter.

Y. That's it. Département 80 (Somme), population 89. The shortest commune name in the world.

Thirteen more have two-letter names. By, Bû, Eu, Gy, Oz, Oô, Py, Ri, Ry, Sy, Ur, Us, Uz. Most are in rural Normandy or the Alps, old enough that medieval scribes only bothered with a syllable. At the other extreme, the longest commune name in France runs to 45 characters: Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson (Marne, population 491). It is, in effect, three communes' worth of name for one village — a relic of the communes nouvelles programme, where neighbouring villages merged but refused to forget who they were.

Names cluster around 8 characters — but the tail is long

The median commune name is 9 characters. The distribution peaks around 7–8 characters — Morlaix, Auxerre, Vesoul — and drops off cleanly on the left. But on the right, the tail stretches all the way to 45. Those outliers are almost all communes nouvelles: villages that merged in the 2010s under administrative reform, hyphenating their former names together rather than choosing one. The result is names that function less like place names and more like complete sentences.

One name in nine belongs to a saint

3,898 communes — 11.1% of the total — begin with Saint or Sainte. France's commune list reads like a medieval liturgical calendar. The leading saint by far is Martin: over 200 communes carry some version of Saint-Martin. Jean, Pierre, Germain, and Julien follow. The Church named France's geography over a thousand years and the Republic never bothered to rename most of it.

The Sainte-Colombe problem

1,450 names are shared by two or more communes — places that happen to have identical official names, living in different départements, separated by hundreds of kilometres. The champion is Sainte-Colombe: 12 different communes bear this exact name, scattered across France from the Hautes-Alpes (population 59) to Seine-et-Marne (population 1,793). If you sent a letter to "Sainte-Colombe, France" with no postal code, the postal service would have twelve guesses.
