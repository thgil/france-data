35,000 communes, and only one is called Y

France's naming obsession: 3,882 saints, 1,449 shared names, and the shortest place name in the world.

2026-05-30 4 min read

France has 35,014 communes. That's more than the rest of the six founding EU nations combined. The names they carry — 32,764 unique ones across 35,014 places — are a compressed archive of medieval religion, landscape features, and the cartographic ambitions of the Revolution. You can read a lot of French history in a name. You can also read a lot of chaos.

One in nine is a saint

Of France's 35,014 communes, 3,882 — exactly 11.1% — carry a saint's name. Saint- (3,587) outnumbers Sainte- (295) twelve to one, because the calendar overwhelmingly favoured male saints when these names were assigned. About 9% of the French population lives in a commune whose name begins Saint-.

The saints themselves repeat endlessly. There are nine communes called Saint-Pierre, nine called Saint-Michel, nine called Le Pin. The postal system has been coping with this since 1852, which is why French addresses insist on the département or the full postal code: the name alone is never enough.

Sainte-Colombe, twelve times over

The single most repeated commune name in France is Sainte-Colombe. Twelve communes share it, spread across twelve different départements — from Ille-et-Vilaine in the northwest to Haute-Saône in the east and Landes in the southwest. Each one traces its name to a third-century martyr from Sens, who allegedly grew her hair back after it was cut. The medieval hagiography machinery ran everywhere and produced the same output.

1,449 distinct names appear in more than one département. That's about one in every 22 unique names. The duplication is not random: it clusters around the same saint names, the same landscape words (Beaulieu, Beaumont, Le Pin), and the same topographic particles (Mont-, Val-, Villeneuve-). Two navigators in different centuries reached for the same words to describe what they saw.

Y

The shortest commune name in France is Y. One letter. A village of 89 people in the Somme, département 80. The name predates French — it comes from a Picard word meaning "water" or "low-lying land." It has been generating address-validation errors in software for decades.

Then come thirteen two-letter communes: Oz, Us, Gy, Sy, Bû, By, Uz, Oô, Ri, Py, Ur, Eu, Ry. Most are equally archaic. Eu in Seine-Maritime has 6,591 residents and a château once visited by Queen Victoria. Oô in Haute-Garonne (103 residents) may be the only place name in France that requires two diacritics.

45 characters

At the other extreme: the longest commune name in France is 45 characters.

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson 45 characters · Marne (51) · pop. 474

It was created by merging three separate communes — Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont, Saint-Genest, and Isson — and simply hyphenating all three names together. France merged thousands of tiny communes in the 1970s under the loi Marcellin. Some of the resulting names are magnificent. The nine next-longest names (38–43 characters each) are also merger products, usually involving two or three saint's names from adjacent valleys.

Le, La, Les

2,140 communes — 6.1% — begin with a definite article: Le (758), La (1,054), or Les (328). La runs slightly ahead because more French nouns are feminine than masculine at the scale of landscape features: la plaine, la forêt, la montagne. These communes typically take their article from the landscape feature they describe, which means they carry grammatical gender as a historical fact about what was there when someone first had to write the name down.

38.3% of all commune names contain a hyphen — they link two or more place-names, landscape words, or saints. The average commune name is 9.7 characters. The median département has between 300 and 500 communes. Together, the 35,014 names are a palimpsest: Gaulish water-words, Latin saints, Norman landscape particles, Revolutionary bureaucratic mergers, and 1970s administrative efficiency all visible in the same list.

A note on the data. Commune names and population figures are drawn from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which covers 34,886 communes and overseas territories (35,014 rows after de-duplication on commune code). Commune boundaries are from the data.gouv.fr Contours administratifs 2025. The communes-index.json derived from this data contains all 35,014 commune names, centroids, and département codes.
