What's in a name? All 35,014 of them.

France has more communes than any other EU country. One is called Y. One has 7 hyphens. Twelve percent begin with Saint.

2026-05-24 5 min read

France has 35,014 communes — more than every other EU member state combined. They range from Paris (over 2 million residents) to hamlets of fewer than a dozen. With that many places to name, things get strange.

The names come from Old French, Latin, Occitan, Breton, Alsatian, and Basque. From saints, lords, rivers, forests, and long-forgotten land boundaries. Then in the 1970s, thousands of tiny communes began to merge under the loi Marcellin — and the newly fused places negotiated names by simply concatenating the old ones, hyphens and all.

The result is a naming register unlike anything else in Europe. Here's what's inside it.

The commune named Y

Y sits in the Somme, between Amiens and Saint-Quentin. Its single-letter name dates back to at least the twelfth century and derives from a Picard root meaning water — there's a river, the Ingon, that passes through. The name is pronounced like the French word i grec (the letter Y), or simply "igrek" by locals.

Y is not alone in the extreme short-name category. France has 12 communes with two-letter names, including Gy (Haute-Saône), By (Doubs), Eu (Seine-Maritime), and Oz (Isère). Another ~130 have names of exactly three characters. Gap (Hautes-Alpes), Die (Drôme), Dax (Landes), Pau (Pyrénées-Atlantiques) — these are small in name but not in size.

The hyphen economy

At the other extreme sits Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson. Forty-five characters. Seven hyphens. Population 491, in the Marne. It was created in 1972 when three communes merged and decided the fairest solution was to keep all three names and chain them together.

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson Marne · 7 hyphens · 45 characters · pop. 491

It's an extreme case, but hyphenated merger names are common. Of France's 35,014 communes, more than 13,000 have at least one hyphen in their name. Twenty-six have five or more. Two have six. And one — the Marne champion — has seven.

Name length distribution

Median: 10 characters. Mean: 11.8 characters. The long tail beyond 30 characters is almost entirely merger names.

If you say you're from Saint-Martin…

France has 32,764 unique commune names — but 35,014 communes. That gap of 2,250 is filled by 1,450 name groups where two or more places share an identical official name.

The most duplicated is Sainte-Colombe, which appears 12 times across France. Saint-Sauveur appears 11 times. Say you're from Saint-Martin — there are 9 communes with that exact name. Say you're from Saint-Pierre — same problem.

In total, roughly 3,700 communes (about 10.6%) share their name with at least one other commune. This is why French administrative addresses always include the département code — the name alone is often ambiguous.

The saint economy

The single most important word in French commune naming is Saint. There are 3,603 communes beginning with Saint- and another 295 beginning with Sainte-, for a combined total of 4,195 — exactly 12.0% of all communes. One in eight French places is named for a saint.

After saints, the next most common first words are directional and geographic: La (496 communes), Le (490), Les (225). Then come repeated landscape words: Villers (105), Villeneuve (70), Beaumont (41), Fontaine (58). France's physical and religious geography is literally encoded in its postal directory.

35,014 points of reference

France has more communes than it knows what to do with — successive governments have tried to reduce the count through mergers, with limited success. The 1971 loi Marcellin created incentives for fusion; the 2015 loi NOTRe created new intercommunality structures. Neither changed the fundamental fact: the French are attached to their communes as units of identity, and the names are part of that.

Y is still Y. Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson still has 7 hyphens. Twelve Sainte-Colombes are still confusing the postal service. The names are a record of every war, every saint, every merger, every river, every stubborn village council that refused to be subsumed.

A note on the data. Commune names and population figures come from DREES's APL 2023 dataset (34,886 communes in metropolitan France and overseas territories with APL scores and 2021 population estimates), which was built on INSEE administrative boundaries. The full dataset of 35,014 communes used here includes the DREES APL communes joined to the Etalab administrative contours (2025 vintage) used in the medical-deserts story. Name lengths, hyphen counts, and duplication analysis are computed from the communeName field in that dataset.
