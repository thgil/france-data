What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes. One is called Y. Twelve are called Sainte-Colombe. 4,443 have "saint" somewhere in their name. And the longest name is 45 characters long.

2026-05-24 4 min read

Every commune in France has an official name. Across 35,014 of them, those names range from single syllables to hyphenated chains that spill off the edge of a letterhead. They encode centuries of geography, hagiography, and municipal merger. And then there is one commune — just one — whose entire official name is a single letter.

Y

Commune in the Somme (département 80) · population 89 · France's only one-letter commune

Y sits in Picardy, in the Somme, population 89. Its name predates the French administrative system by centuries; the etymology is thought to be Celtic or Germanic water-word. It is, by any measure, the most efficiently named place in France.

One step up, thirteen communes have two-letter names — Eu, Gy, Ry, Us, Oô, and eight others — each the product of the same deep compression between a name's sound and the alphabet's limits. Eu (Seine-Maritime) is the largest of them, population 6,591. Uz (Hautes-Pyrénées) is the smallest, with 36 residents.

Twelve Sainte-Colombes

At the other end of the uniqueness spectrum: France has 32,764 distinct commune names spread across 35,014 communes. That gap of 2,250 means exactly 1,450 names are shared by at least two different communes. If you tell someone you live in Sainte-Colombe, they would need to ask which one — there are twelve.

The duplicates aren't accidents — they're geography layered on hagiography. Saint-Sauveur (11 communes), Saint-Aubin (10), Beaulieu (10), Saint-Marcel (9): these are the patron saints and topographic descriptions that medieval parishes independently reached for across France. Different Sainte-Colombes, same sainted martyr.

One in nine communes is a saint

3,882 communes whose name starts with Saint- or Sainte-
4,443 communes with "saint" anywhere in their name

11.1% of French communes begin with Saint- or Sainte-. Add in the communes that embed a saint mid-name — Fontenay-Saint-Père, Beaumont-Saint-Cyr, La Vacquerie-et-Saint-Martin-de-Castries — and 4,443 communes, one in eight, carry a saint somewhere in their official name. The word martin alone appears in 255 commune names; jean in 170; pierre in 180.

The secular revolution renamed some saints. Most survived it. Topography proved more durable than politics: the saints are still there, in the cadastre, in the IGN map tiles, in the national address database.

The distribution of a name

The average commune name is 11.8 characters long. The modal length is 7 or 8 characters — enough for one syllable and a hyphen, or a two-syllable word. Most names cluster between 5 and 15 characters; the long tail belongs to the fused communes, created when municipalities merged and neither partner was willing to yield its name.

Name length in characters (1 = Y, 45 = Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-…). Source: DREES APL 2023 commune list via Etalab.

45 characters

The longest commune name in France belongs to a merged municipality in the Marne:

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson 45 characters · Marne (51) · population 491

It is the product of a commune nouvelle, the wave of voluntary mergers that has reshaped France's municipal map since 2010. When three villages merge, their names often get hyphenated together — a democratic compromise between erasure and preservation. The Haute-Saône holds second place: Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (43 characters, population 923). The town of Roche-sur-Linotte-et-Sorans-les-Cordiers in the same département rounds out the top four with 40 characters and 64 residents.

These names are a kind of democratic scar tissue. Every hyphen is a negotiation.

-ville, -court, -sur-

The endings are a linguistic stratigraphy. -ville (1,005 communes) is the most common suffix — a Germanic wil meaning settlement, absorbed into Old French. -court (700 communes) is Frankish for courtyard or enclosure, dense in Normandy and Picardy where Frankish settlement was heaviest. -sur-Mer appears 99 times along every coast. -les-Bains 60 times, wherever there is a thermal spring worth naming. The endings are a map of conquest, commerce, and tourism laid over the same patch of ground.

35,014 names. One letter, forty-five. A saint, a valley, a thermal spring, a merger. Each one is a compressed act of naming that someone, at some point, thought was the right description of where they lived.

A note on the data. Commune names and codes come from the DREES APL 2023 commune file, joined to Etalab administrative boundaries (2025 vintage). Coverage is 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Name analysis uses communeName from the APL dataset. Length counts are character-level (not byte-level), so accented characters count as one.
