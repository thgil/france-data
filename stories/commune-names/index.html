What's in a name?

France has 35,014 communes with names ranging from one letter to forty-five. Here's what the full list reveals.

2026-05-13 5 min read

Somewhere in the Somme, 49.806°N 2.985°E, there is a French commune called Y.

Y
Dept. Somme (80), code 80829
Population: 89  ·  1 character
One of the shortest official place names in Europe

That's not a data-entry error or a postal abbreviation. Y is the commune's legal name, registered in the Code Officiel Géographique since the Revolution. The name predates the administrative system itself — it derives from the Latin Ica, via Picard dialect. Once a small river ran through it. The river is gone. The name stayed.

Y sits among a small club of ultra-short French commune names. Thirteen communes have two-letter names: Eu (Seine-Maritime, pop. 6,591), Oô (Haute-Garonne, with its double circumflex), Py, Ri, Oz, Sy, Uz, Ry, Us, By, Gy, Bû, Ur. Only Y has one.

The saint empire

Of France's 35,014 communes, exactly 3,882 — one in nine — have names that begin with Saint or Sainte. That's more communes than any other European country has in total. The saints are not evenly distributed.

Ardèche leads by share: 29% of its 335 communes are named after a saint. In absolute terms, Dordogne is the champion with 128 — a quarter of its communes. The pattern follows the old Gallo-Roman ecclesiastical map: the south-west, the Rhône valley, and the Atlantic coast were densely Christianised early, and their commune names fossilised around the parish church.

The name wars

France has 35,014 communes but far fewer distinct names. The most duplicated: Sainte-Colombe, shared by twelve separate villages. If you live in any of them and receive a letter addressed simply to "Sainte-Colombe, France", it could go to any of eleven other places.

Most-duplicated commune names

Count of communes sharing each exact name. Source: INSEE COG via DREES APL dataset.

Eleven communes are called Saint-Sauveur, ten share Saint-Aubin, ten share Beaulieu. Even the secular names repeat: nine communes are called Le Pin, eight called Beaumont. France distinguishes them by appending hyphens and context: Saint-Sauveur-en-Puisaye (Yonne), Saint-Sauveur-de-Montagut (Ardèche), Saint-Sauveur-le-Vicomte (Manche). The disambiguation is built into the name itself.

45 characters, 7 hyphens, 491 residents

Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson
Marne (51)  ·  45 characters  ·  7 hyphens  ·  pop. 491

France's longest commune name belongs to a village in the Marne. It wasn't always this way: in 1972, three neighbouring communes — Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont, Saint-Genest, and Isson — merged under the loi Marcellin, which encouraged rural communes to consolidate. Rather than choose one name, they concatenated all three. The hyphen became a democratic instrument.

The runner-up: Beaujeu-Saint-Vallier-Pierrejux-et-Quitteur (Haute-Saône, 43 characters), another 1972 merger. The top ten longest names nearly all share this origin: they are the administrative scar tissue of post-war rural consolidation, seven or eight syllables of compromise preserved in the official record.

The median commune name is 10 characters long. The distribution is right-skewed: most names are short, but a long tail of merger compounds pulls the average up to 11.8. Of 35,014 commune names, 13,410 (38.3%) contain at least one hyphen — a share that reflects both the French administrative norm of hyphenating multi-word names and the mid-century merger wave that strung existing names together.

Names are data too. France's commune registry is a palimpsest: you can read the Roman occupation in the -ac and -y suffixes (Meaux, Poissy, Y itself), the Frankish settlement in -ville and -court, the Christian parish network in every Saint- and Sainte-, and the 1970s rural policy in every seven-hyphen merger name. It's the longest-running continuous address book in the world.

A note on the data. Commune names and codes come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which joins INSEE's Code Officiel Géographique (COG) to GP-access scores. This gives 35,014 communes including overseas territories. Name-length and pattern analyses were run in Python against the full dataset; character counts use Python's len() on the raw Unicode strings. The communes-index.json file in this story folder (35,014 entries, 3 MB) is available for reuse by other stories in this project.
