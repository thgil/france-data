France has 202 Saint-Martins

One in nine French communes is named after a saint. The most popular saint appears 202 times. The shortest commune name is a single letter.

2026-05-17 4 min read

France has 35,014 communes — more than any other country in the European Union. Germany, with a larger population and territory, has roughly 11,000. This is a legacy of the Revolution: in 1789, the National Assembly turned every parish into a commune, and most of them are still there, unchanged. The names that stuck, though, came from somewhere much older.

The saint leaderboard

Of France's 35,014 communes, 3,882 — 11.1% — begin with Saint- or Sainte-. The chart below shows the top 15 by the first saint name in the compound. Saint-Martin is the clear leader. Martin of Tours, the Roman soldier who cut his cloak to share with a beggar, became the patron saint of France, and the geography has not forgotten him.

Communes whose name begins with each saint (top 15)

Saint-Jean (155) and Saint-Pierre (144) follow. Then Saint-Germain (108) — a bishop of Auxerre who became enormously popular in the early Middle Ages. Together the top four saints account for 609 commune names: roughly one parish in every 57 across France wears one of these four names.

The name distribution is not just top-heavy with saints — it's also wildly asymmetric at the extremes. France has a commune whose entire name is the letter Y (Somme, population 89). It has another whose name runs to 45 characters. The average is a surprisingly tidy 11.8 characters.

The hyphen and the merger

One more pattern in the data: 38.3% of commune names contain at least one hyphen — 13,410 out of 35,014. The hyphen is France's bureaucratic inheritance from the nineteenth century, when hundreds of small communes were merged under compound names. The formula is usually original-village-de-the-river-or-feature-nearby. The longest names are the extreme expression of this logic — some communes absorbed two or three neighbours and kept everyone's name in the deed.

The saints, the letters, the hyphens: the commune name list is a 35,000-entry index of French history, from the Roman roads that Saint-Martin walked to the Revolution that turned every parish into a republic in miniature.

A note on the data. Commune names and codes come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which covers 35,014 communes across metropolitan France and overseas territories. Saint prefix counts were derived by extracting the first two hyphenated segments of each commune name. The commune Y (Somme, INSEE code 80829) is real — it has been a commune since the French Revolution.
