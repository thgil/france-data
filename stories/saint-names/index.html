One letter, 3,882 saints

France has 35,014 communes. One is called Y. One in nine starts with Saint. This is what naming a country looks like.

2026-05-21 4 min read

Somewhere in the Somme, between Amiens and Saint-Quentin, there is a village called Y. Not Wy or Why — just Y. It has 89 residents, a church, and the shortest commune name in France. If you want to write it in full, you already have.

Y is not alone. France has 13 communes with names of just two letters: Bû (Eure-et-Loir, 2,022 residents), Eu (Seine-Maritime, 6,591), Gy (Haute-Saône, 1,001), and ten others. They survived centuries of administrative reform, two republics, the Revolution, the Vichy regime, and the 2016 municipal mergers. Their brevity is a kind of stubbornness.

One in nine, a saint

France has 3,882 communes whose names begin with Saint or Sainte — 11.1% of all communes. They are the single largest naming category in the country, by a wide margin. The secular republic did not rename them. The Revolution tried — Saint-Tropez briefly became Héraclée — and failed. The saints won.

Among them, Saint-Martin leads with 202 separate communes, followed by Saint-Jean (155) and Saint-Pierre (144). These are not just places that happen to share a name: they are geographically dispersed, historically independent villages that were each founded, dedicated, and administered under the same patron. The top 15 saint names account for nearly 1,400 communes on their own.

Twelve Sainte-Colombes

The most duplicated commune name in France is Sainte-Colombe — 12 places, spread across Ardèche, Charente-Maritime, Doubs, Gironde, Ille-et-Vilaine, and seven other departments. They share nothing but the name and, presumably, the same long-gone local church. If you send a letter to "Sainte-Colombe, France" with no department code, it will be undeliverable. The postal system solved this with five-digit INSEE codes. The names were never disambiguated.

Saint-Sauveur appears 11 times, Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu ten times each. Of France's 35,014 commune names, 2,250 are shared with at least one other commune. That is a 6.4% duplication rate — a measure of how thoroughly the same religious and geographic vocabulary shaped independent medieval naming decisions across the whole territory.

The 45-character commune

At the opposite end from Y is Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson. Forty-five characters. It is a merged commune in the Marne, population 491, formed in 1972 from several villages that insisted on keeping all their names. The hyphen-and-et construction is the administrative grammar of French municipal mergers: two or more old names, joined with hyphens and preserved in full. The country has villages with 40-plus character names, and they are all small, all merged, and all stubborn about their heritage.

The shape of a name

Most French commune names are 6–19 characters long. The distribution peaks sharply around 7–8 characters — the length of a simple village name like Auxerre (7) or Chartres (8) — then thins into a long tail of hyphenated compound names. The average is 11.8 characters. Y sits alone at the left edge of the distribution, a single column, 89 people.

Distribution of commune name lengths

The naming of France is a stratigraphy — Gaulish roots under Latin saints under Revolutionary rebranding under merger bureaucracy. Each layer is still visible. Y is probably pre-Roman, a monosyllabic place-name from an extinct language that survived because it was too small to bother renaming. Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson is a 1972 administrative compromise. Both are official. Both have INSEE codes. Both get their rubbish collected.

A note on the data. Commune names and populations come from the APL (Accessibilité Potentielle Localisée) dataset published by DREES, which covers all 35,014 French communes including overseas territories. Population figures are INSEE 2021 estimates. Name analysis was computed from the communeName field across all features. Name lengths are measured in characters on the official commune name string as it appears in the INSEE administrative register.
