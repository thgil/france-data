What's in a name?

France has a commune called Y. It also has one called Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson. Both are real. Between them lie 35,014 communes and every naming quirk in the book.

2026-05-18 4 min read

Y is a commune in the Somme, département 80, population 89. Its name is one character long. It is the shortest place name in France, and one of the shortest in the world for any inhabited settlement. The post office accepts it. The INSEE code is 80829. If you search for it online, you will have a bad time.

At the other end of the spectrum: Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson, a commune in the Marne (département 51) with 259 residents and 45 characters in its name. The average French commune name is 11.8 characters long. The distribution below tells the full story.

Commune name lengths (35,014 communes)

The distribution peaks sharply around 7–8 characters — Beaumont, Le Pin, Fontaine. It has a long right tail: 122 communes have names longer than 30 characters, almost all of them compound names created when small villages were merged together under the loi Marcellin of 1971.

One in eight communes is a saint

4,443 of France's 35,014 communes — 12.7% of the total — contain the word saint or sainte somewhere in their name. 3,587 begin with Saint-; another 295 begin with Sainte-. France has more saint-communes than most countries have communes, period.

4,443 communes named after a saint — 12.7% of all French communes

The saint communes span the full alphabet. Every common saint gets multiple villages: there are 10 communes called Saint-Aubin, 9 called Saint-Pierre, 9 called Saint-Marcel, 9 called Saint-Michel. Beyond the saints, other prefixes proliferate: La Chapelle- appears 147 times, Villers- 103 times, Villeneuve- 64 times.

There are twelve Sainte-Colombes

The single most repeated commune name in France is Sainte-Colombe, which appears 12 times. These twelve villages share an identical name and are spread across 12 different departments. If you write a letter to "Sainte-Colombe, France" — no department code, no INSEE number — it is genuinely ambiguous. Postal workers manage. Somehow.

Runner-up is Saint-Sauveur with 11, followed by Saint-Aubin and Beaulieu with 10 each. In total, 1,450 distinct commune names are shared by more than one village — covering 3,700 communes altogether. That's more than one in ten.

The alphabet's edge cases

Below Y, there are 13 two-character commune names. Most end in a vowel: Sy, Py, By, Ry are Normandy relics of Norse settlement, their names worn down to near-nothing by a thousand years of use. Eu (Seine-Maritime, pop. 7,400) is the largest two-letter commune. Oô (Haute-Garonne, pop. 48) is the strangest-looking. Bû wears a circumflex.

These tiny names are not typos or abbreviations. They are the surviving sediment of Old French, Occitan, Basque, Breton, and Norse — place names that were already short when they were first written down, and have only shortened since. The French state, which assigns each commune a five-digit INSEE code regardless of name length, treats Y exactly the same as Saint-Remy-en-Bouzemont-Saint-Genest-et-Isson. One column, one row, one commune.

A note on the data. Commune names and populations come from the DREES APL 2023 dataset, which covers 35,014 French communes (metropolitan France and overseas territories). Name-length analysis, duplicate counting, and prefix aggregation were computed from that dataset. The APL file was originally built for the medical deserts story; the commune names it carries are the official INSEE names as of the 2023 edition.
