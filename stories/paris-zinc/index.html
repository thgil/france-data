Paris au zinc

3,120 bars, 20 arrondissements, a 15× gap. The bar map of Paris is the social map of Paris.

2026-05-12 4 min read

Paris has 3,120 registered débits de boissons — bars, cafés, and brasseries licensed to serve alcohol on the premises. That's 14.6 per 10,000 residents, roughly twice France's national average. But Paris is not one city; it's twenty. And the distribution of those 3,120 bars is anything but even.

The smallest arrondissement is the loudest

The 1er arrondissement has 15,919 permanent residents — fewer than many a suburban commune. It also has 123 bars: 77 per 10,000 residents, the highest density in Paris and ten times the national average. Châtelet, Les Halles, the Marais fringe — these streets were never designed for quiet mornings. Each day the population multiplies several times over with workers, shoppers, and tourists. The zinc follows the footfall, not the census.

The 2e (61/10k) and the 8e (44/10k) share the same logic: small residential footprints, enormous commercial traffic. The 8e adds a further distinction — it also has the highest pharmacy density in Paris (16.5 per 10,000), a finding that echoes the pharmacy-myth story's observation about the Champs-Élysées district.

The 11e: absolute champion

By raw count, no arrondissement comes close to the 11e: 386 bars. Bastille, Oberkampf, Faubourg Saint-Antoine, the Canal Saint-Martin fringe. This is the arrondissement that gave the world rue Oberkampf as a synonym for late-night Paris. With 142,583 residents, it's large enough that the density figure — 27 per 10,000 — is moderate by central Paris standards. But the absolute number is the story: one bar for every 369 residents. No other arrondissement concentrates that much socialising infrastructure.

Passy stays home

The 16e arrondissement has 162,061 residents, 83 bars, and the lowest density in Paris: 5.1 bars per 10,000. That is below the national French average of roughly 7.3 per 10,000 residents. The wealthiest arrondissement of the French capital has fewer bars per resident than the average département in the provinces.

The 7e has the fewest bars by absolute count — just 63 — despite being home to the Eiffel Tower, the Musée d'Orsay, the National Assembly, and dozens of foreign embassies. The 7e is a place of government and monuments, not neighbourhood bistros.

The pattern is stable and old. Haussmann's mid-19th-century renovation of Paris displaced the working class eastward, concentrating industry, density, and daily commerce in the arrondissements east of République. The bar map hasn't forgotten. The 13e, 14e, 15e, and 16e — the bourgeois south and west — all sit in the bottom half of the chart, below 8 bars per 10,000. The 10e, 11e, 18e, and 20e all sit above the Paris average.

The café outlasts the doctor

The 19e arrondissement sits near the bottom of the bar-density chart (7.8/10k), but it occupies a more significant position in one other ranking: it has the fewest pharmacies per resident of any Paris arrondissement, at 2.5 per 10,000. La Villette, Belleville, Jaurès — the 19e has been identified in earlier stories as one of Paris's patches of medical under-service. The bars persist; the doctors are scarcer. It is the same pattern as the national one: in France's most stretched communities, the zinc tends to outlast the surgery.

Paris in context

France's most bar-dense département is Haute-Corse, at around 21 bars per 10,000 residents — a figure shaped by tourism, a culture of village squares, and a small permanent population. Paris overall (14.6/10k) sits comfortably above the national average but well below Corsica. Within Paris, the 1er (77/10k) would rank among France's most bar-dense ski resort communes. The 16e (5.1/10k) would rank among the quieter rural départements of the interior.

That 15× spread — from 5.1 to 77 bars per 10,000 residents — inside a single city tells you something that no département-level map can: Paris is the most unequal city in France, and the bar is one of the few places where you can still see the seams.

Notes

Population figures are the arrondissement resident population (recensement 2023 estimate). Bar density per 10,000 is bars ÷ resident population × 10,000. The 1er, 2e, 8e, and to a lesser degree the 9e have large daytime populations from commuters and tourists not counted in the resident figure; their density ratios are thus inflated relative to true daily usage.
