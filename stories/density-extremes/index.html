Packed and empty

Paris 11e has 42,000 people per km² — four times Manhattan. A single commune in French Guiana is larger than Kuwait. Both are French.

2026-05-08 4 min read

France has 35,000 communes — and they are not alike. Most people imagine France as a country of modest villages and mid-size provincial cities, with Paris as the conspicuous exception. The population density data tells a more extreme story: a republic of outliers, where some places are among the most crowded on Earth and others approach the emptiness of the Sahara.

The most densely populated commune in France (among those with at least 1 km² of area) is Paris 11e Arrondissement: 142,583 people in 3.36 km². That works out to 42,494 people per km² — more than four times the density of New York City (approximately 10,900/km²), and roughly twice Barcelona (15,970/km²). The 11e is not the grandest arrondissement — no Opéra, no Louvre — but it is one of the most alive, with its markets, its bistrots, and its impossibly dense street grid.

The suburbs give Paris a run

The 15 densest communes include 9 Paris arrondissements — but also Vincennes (Val-de-Marne, 35,592/km²), Levallois-Perret (Hauts-de-Seine, 28,265/km²), Montrouge (Hauts-de-Seine, 27,289/km²), Boulogne-Billancourt (Hauts-de-Seine, 23,313/km²), and Lyon 1er (Rhône, 23,043/km²). Dense urban France is not confined to the périphérique.

Vincennes: 35,592 people/km². The commune sits adjacent to the Bois de Vincennes — France's largest urban park, 995 hectares of forest and lakes. Yet Vincennes itself packs 48,798 people into 1.37 km² and edges out Paris 18e, Paris 19e, and Paris 20e. The Château de Vincennes is at one end of the commune; the rest is dense Haussmann-style residential fabric.

And then there is Guyane

At the other end of the distribution sits French Guiana — the overseas territory grouped as département 97 in France's administrative system. Here, the word "commune" takes on an entirely different meaning.

Maripasoula: 18,716 km², population 9,177. That is 0.49 people per km². For comparison, Kuwait covers 17,818 km² and has 4.3 million inhabitants. Maripasoula is a single French commune, larger in area than an OECD member state, with fewer people than a modest French market town. It is also a medical desert (APL 0.00).

Saint-Élie holds the density record for the least crowded: 241 people spread across 5,806 km² — 0.042 people per km². The Sahara Desert averages roughly 1.2 per km². Saint-Élie is 28 times emptier than the Sahara.

Régina (Guyane): 1,655 people, 11,361 km². Larger than Jamaica (10,991 km²). Its population would fill roughly a quarter of a Paris arrondissement.

Outside Guyane, the emptiest metropolitan communes are in the high Alps. Saint-Christophe-en-Oisans (Isère) covers 239 km² of mountains and glaciers with 102 people — 0.43/km². Saint-Paul-sur-Ubaye (Alpes-de-Haute-Provence) has 176 people across 209 km² of the Ubaye valley.

One republic, a million-to-one ratio

Within the French Republic, the extremes of population density span roughly 1,000,000 to 1: from Paris 11e at ~42,000/km² to Saint-Élie at 0.042/km². No other country of comparable size contains such a range within a single national territory — a consequence of France's colonial geography, which extended its administrative borders deep into equatorial rainforest.

The median French commune has about 49 people per km² — roughly the density of rural Ireland or the Scottish Highlands. That median feels intuitive if you drive through the Massif Central, the Sologne, or the Champagne plains. Paris, by contrast, is not just a city — it is a statistical outlier so extreme it barely belongs on the same chart as the rest of the country.

Lozère, the least dense metropolitan département, has 16.3 people per km² — four times the density of Guyane. The diagonale du vide — the empty diagonal from the Meuse to the Pyrenees — is well-documented, but it looks almost crowded next to the Guyane communes that bookend France's density distribution.

The numbers above are approximate: commune polygon boundaries in this dataset are simplified at 1000 m resolution, which means computed areas for very small or intricate geometries (Côte d'Azur coastal communes, complex Alpine boundaries) carry some error. The rankings for large communes — Paris arrondissements, the Guyane giants — are robust. The precise figures for sub-km² communes are not.

A note on the data. Population figures are from DREES APL 2023, drawing on INSEE 2021 census estimates embedded in the commune file. Polygon geometries are from Etalab's Contours administratifs communes 2025 at 1000 m simplification. Areas are computed as signed spherical areas using the WGS84 ellipsoid (approximate). Communes with computed area below 1 km² are excluded from the dense ranking to avoid inflated density values caused by polygon simplification; they remain in the sparse ranking where the direction of error does not affect the result. All 35,014 communes with non-zero population are included in the underlying dataset; this story filters to population ≥ 100 for legibility.
