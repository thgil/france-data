The baguette capital

7,378 bakeries, 1,286 communes, and one clear winner — Paris 1er at 21 boulangeries per 10,000 residents.

2026-05-20 4 min read

Île-de-France has 7,378 bakeries spread across 1,286 communes. The regional average is 6.0 per 10,000 residents — one boulangerie for every 1,667 people. But averages tell you almost nothing here. The true distribution spans a factor of more than three, from commercial-dense Paris arrondissements to the 582 communes (45 per cent of IDF) that have no bakery at all.

Why Paris 1er wins

Paris 1er Arrondissement holds 34 bakeries for 15,919 residents — 21.4 per 10,000. That's 3.5 times the IDF average. But this isn't just about resident population: the 1er hosts the Louvre, Les Halles, and the city's highest daytime foot traffic. Bakeries here serve commuters, tourists, and office workers as much as they serve neighbours.

Eight of the top 20 are Paris arrondissements. The exceptions are interesting: Hardricourt (78) and Houdan (78) are small market towns where a handful of boulangeries serve an agricultural hinterland larger than their INSEE population count suggests. Arnouville (95) — 14.4 per 10,000 — is a dense suburban commune with an unusually high bakery-to-resident ratio for its size class.

Density vs size

Below: every IDF commune that has at least one bakery and at least 500 residents, plotted by population (log scale, horizontal) against bakeries per 10,000 (vertical). The dashed line is the IDF average.

Paris arrondissements cluster in the top-right: large populations and high densities simultaneously. No other communes manage that combination. Small golden dots in the upper left are rural communes where one or two boulangeries produce high per-capita figures by serving a population of 600 — not evidence of unusual bakery culture, just arithmetic.

By département

Paris (75) is the only IDF département to break 9 bakeries per 10,000. The inner suburbs — Hauts-de-Seine (92), Val-de-Marne (94), Seine-Saint-Denis (93) — cluster around 5–6. The outer ring falls below the IDF average.

The bakery desert

582 of 1,286 IDF communes — 45 per cent — have no recorded bakery. Most are small exurban or rural communes in Seine-et-Marne (77) and the outer Yvelines (78), where residents drive to a neighbouring town for their baguette. This pattern mirrors the medical desert geography: the same commercial and demographic pressures that keep GP access uneven also keep bread access uneven. The baguette-free zone and the doctor-free zone overlap on the same map.

The implication is straightforward: a bakery, like a pharmacy and a doctor, is a commercial service that responds to foot traffic and purchasing power. It clusters in dense urban cores and retreats from low-density peripheries. Paris 1er wins the per-capita baguette competition not because Parisians love bread more than people in Seine-et-Marne, but because density compounds density.

A note on the data. Bakery locations come from the same INSEE SIRENE pipeline used in Story 3 (baguettes): 7,378 geocoded active boulangeries (NAF 1071C) in Île-de-France as of March 2026. Population figures are from DREES APL 2023 commune-level data (INSEE 2021 population base). The per-capita calculation uses only communes with a recorded positive population; communes in the APL file with null or zero population are excluded from density rankings. The 2,000-resident threshold for the main ranking chart filters communes where small-denominator effects dominate.
