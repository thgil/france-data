Génicourt is the baguette capital

7,378 bakeries in Île-de-France — but which commune has the most per resident? Dividing bread by people changes everything.

2026-05-22 4 min read

The baguettes story counted 7,378 bakeries across Île-de-France and mapped each one as a gold dot. Paris 18e topped the list with 192 — the most of any commune. But absolute counts favour big communes by definition. A more honest question: relative to the people who live there and presumably eat bread, where is Île-de-France's bread supply actually richest?

To answer that, we join the SIRENE bakery locations (by commune code) to the 2021 INSEE census population — and compute bakeries per 10,000 residents for every commune that has at least one.

Small towns dominate the top of the list

Plot every commune with a bakery on axes of population (log scale) and per-capita density, and a pattern emerges immediately: the top-left corner — tiny populations — is where the per-capita numbers get extreme. Génicourt, Val-d'Oise, has 513 residents and two bakeries. That works out to 39 per 10,000 — nearly four times the median.

These numbers are fragile. Génicourt loses one bakery and falls to 19. But they aren't random: rural villages on the Seine-et-Marne and Yvelines fringes consistently keep two or three boulangeries alive for populations well under 1,000. The village bakery is a social institution, not just a commercial one — which is partly why it survives where a supermarket wouldn't.

Among large communes, Paris 1er — and a surprise from Val-d'Oise

Once you filter to communes with at least 5,000 residents — where the ratios stabilise into something meaningful — a clearer picture emerges. Paris 1er leads at 21 bakeries per 10,000. The 2nd, 3rd, and 8th arrondissements follow. The historic right-bank core has the densest bread supply in the region by far.

But at #4 sits Arnouville, a Val-d'Oise suburb of 14,585 people with 21 bakeries — 14.4 per 10,000. It beats sixteen of Paris's twenty arrondissements. Further down the outer-department list, Souppes-sur-Loing (Seine-et-Marne) and Beauchamp (Val-d'Oise) both top 13 per 10,000. Neither is famous for its bread.

The top 25 — where small-town bread wins

Filtering to communes with at least 500 residents (to reduce noise from the single-bakery villages) and ranking by per-10,000 gives a list dominated by small communes in Seine-et-Marne, Yvelines, and Val-d'Oise — the rural and peri-urban fringe. Paris 1er enters at #19, the first Paris arrondissement on the list.

The baguette deserts

581 of Île-de-France's 1,285 communes — 45% — have zero registered bakeries. These aren't only tiny hamlets; many are mid-size suburban communes where car-based retail planning replaced the village high street. Épinay-sous-Sénart (Essonne, pop 11,869) has one bakery: 0.8 per 10,000. Les Ulis (Essonne, pop 25,253) has three: 1.2 per 10,000. Carrières-sur-Seine (Yvelines, pop 15,038) has two: 1.3 per 10,000.

The same diagonale du vide that runs through medical access and bar density shows up here, too — but in IDF it takes a different form. The underserved communes aren't the most remote; they're the planned post-war and new-town suburbs, built for the car, where the boulangerie de quartier never took root. The geography of bread is the geography of walkability.

Paris 18e has 192 bakeries and nobody doubts it lives up to its reputation. But Génicourt (pop 513, Val-d'Oise) has two — and per resident, it's the baguette capital of Île-de-France. The smallest town on the map sometimes has the richest table.

A note on the data. Bakery locations are from INSEE SIRENE (NAF code 1071C — boulangeries and boulangeries-pâtisseries), active establishments as of a March 2026 snapshot, geocoded via SIRENE Géolocalisation. Population figures are from the 2021 INSEE communal census (recensement de la population), sourced from the DREES APL dataset which uses 2021 population as its denominator. Île-de-France comprises départements 75, 77, 78, 91, 92, 93, 94, 95. 7,378 bakeries mapped across 1,285 communes with population data; 704 communes have at least one.
