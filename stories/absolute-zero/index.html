Absolute zero

537 communes where the official GP accessibility score is not low, not scarce — it is exactly 0.00. Corsica holds 146 of them.

May 2026 5 min read

France's official measure of GP access is the accessibilité potentielle localisée, or APL. A score of 2.5 is the government's threshold for a "medical desert." A score of 1.0 defines severe underservice. Both figures imply that something is there — just not enough. Below 1.0, the supply exists but is barely reachable.

And then there is 0.00. Not rounding down. Not a data artefact. The APL model, which weights catchment distance and standardised demand, outputs zero when it finds no accessible GP supply at all. France has 537 communes where this is the case — not 537 communes with low access, but 537 where the number is literally zero.

The island

If you mapped every APL=0 commune in France and counted them by département, the answer at the top of the list would not be Guyane, or Lozère, or any corner of the Massif Central. It would be Haute-Corse. Département 2B holds 116 APL=0 communes — more than a fifth of the national total, from a territory of 236 municipalities. Add Corse-du-Sud (2A, 30 communes) and the island as a whole accounts for 146, or 27% of every commune in France where official GP access is zero.

The reason is geography. Corsica's interior is a landscape of mountain villages on limestone ridges, connected by single-lane roads that become impassable in heavy winter snow. Many of these communes are tiny: Érone, in the Castagniccia, has 12 residents. Tarrano has 15. Piedipartino, 16. Piobetta, 17. They elect mayors, maintain registries, hold communal elections — but the nearest GP practice may require an hour through mountain switchbacks. The APL model, which measures what a standardised resident can reach, finds nothing.

The southern arc

Outside Corsica, the zeros trace a consistent geographic logic: mountains and southern margins. Aude (38 communes) sweeps from the Pyrenean foothills to the Languedoc plateau. Drôme and Ardèche — the pre-Alpine corridor east of the Rhône — contribute 51 between them. Pyrénées-Orientales, Gard, and Aveyron add another 54. The Massif Central heartland (Lozère, Haute-Loire, Cantal, Ariège) brings 61 more.

Alpes-Maritimes is the most surprising entry at third place nationally: 36 APL=0 communes in a département famous for the Côte d'Azur, Nice, and Monaco. The explanation is its back-country. Zoom to the map's Alpes-Maritimes preset and you'll find the zeroes in the hills above Menton — Gorbio (1,527 residents), Sainte-Agnès (1,351), Castellar (1,087), Utelle (844). These are postcard villages with panoramic views of the Mediterranean. They don't have a GP.

The surprises

The median APL=0 commune has 101 residents, and 263 of the 537 have under 100. But not all APL=0 communes are mountain hamlets. Meymac, in Corrèze (Limousin), has 2,279 residents — a small town with a covered market, a medieval tower, and a weekly newspaper. Saugues in Haute-Loire (1,681 residents) is a stop on the GR65, the pilgrimage route to Santiago. These are not micro-villages where the population shrank away: they are functioning small towns whose last GP has retired and not been replaced. The APL model, indifferent to the town's self-image, duly outputs zero.

Globally, the 13 APL=0 communes with more than 1,000 residents hold a combined 44,521 people. Add the Guyane communes — Maripasoula (9,177), Grand-Santi (9,120), Papaichton (5,606), accessible primarily by river pirogue or light aircraft — and the scale of the "absolute zero" population is clearer: 110,880 people, roughly the size of Poitiers, with no calculable GP access at all.

What zero means

The APL is a statistical model, not a direct count. A score of zero doesn't mean a commune has literally never seen a doctor — it means the weighted supply, accounting for distance and competing demand from neighbouring communes, is mathematically zero within the catchment model's parameters. Residents in APL=0 communes still see doctors: they drive further, they rely more heavily on emergency services, they wait longer. The number is abstract. The consequences aren't.

France's 1,748 "severe" communes (APL under 1.0) hold 478,895 people. The 537 APL=0 communes are a subset of that, the innermost ring of the access deficit. They appear across every kind of French territory — Corsican ridge villages, back-country Côte d'Azur, Massif Central market towns, equatorial forest settlements — bound by a single fact: the model that France uses to measure its own healthcare access can find no supply at all.

Whether a score of 0.00 is the same problem as a score of 0.1 or 0.8 is a question for clinicians and policymakers. What the data confirms is that the official system has named 537 places and assigned them the same number: zero. They are not the same as each other. They share a threshold.

Method

APL=0 communes were identified by filtering the DREES dataset for an exact value of 0.00. The 537 communes include both metropolitan France and overseas territories (grouped as département 97 in this data release). Corsica (2A, 2B) is counted as part of France's metropolitan scope. Map geometry is a 192 KB subset of the full Etalab commune boundaries, filtered to APL=0 communes only to reduce load time.
