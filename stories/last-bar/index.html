The last bar standing

Bars and doctors are barely correlated across France. In the rural heartland, the café often outlasts the surgery. 7.4 million people live in a commune with a bar but no GP.

2026-05-08 5 min read

We've mapped France's 49,385 bars. We've mapped its 12,771 medical deserts. Now cross the two datasets and something unexpected appears: knowing whether a commune has a bar tells you almost nothing about whether it has a doctor.

At the département level, the correlation between bar density and medical access is 0.19 — barely above zero. The zinc counter and the cabinet médical are governed by entirely different logics.

Of the 8,320 medical desert communes (pop ≥ 200), 35% — some 2,950 places — still have at least one registered bar. Their 7.4 million residents can order a demi but wait weeks for a GP appointment.

The rural kind: the café stays when everything else goes

In the French countryside, the bar closes last. The post office merged, the primary school consolidated with the next village, the baker retired and nobody took over. But the café — the last place to have a coffee, a game of cards, sometimes an informal community bulletin board — holds on.

Châteauneuf-de-Randon, in Lozère: 523 residents, two bars, APL score of 0.00. Meymac, in Corrèze: 2,279 residents, three bars, APL of 0.00. Saugues, in Haute-Loire: 1,681 residents, four bars, APL of 0.00. The APL model has no GP consultations to count here. But the bars are still open.

The suburban kind: bars stayed, doctors never came

Argenteuil, in Val-d'Oise: 107,000 people, 46 bars, APL of 1.74. A major city — France's 20th largest commune — with a functioning urban culture, restaurants, transport. And officially a medical desert.

The outer ring of Paris dominates this list: Sarcelles, Villejuif, Bobigny, Sartrouville. These are not empty villages — they are dense, functioning towns that grew faster than the medical infrastructure that was supposed to serve them. The bars came with the population boom. The GPs did not.

At département level: almost no relationship

Zoom out to the 96 metropolitan and Corsican départements and plot bars per capita against medical access. The correlation is 0.19: knowing how many bars a département has tells you almost nothing about its APL score.

Bars per 10,000 residents vs medical access score, by département

A few outliers tell the real story. Cher (18): 8.5 bars per 10,000 residents — above the national average. APL of 2.25 — the worst in mainland France, with 60% of its residents in a medical desert. The bars stayed. The doctors left. Bas-Rhin (67): 3.9 bars per 10,000 — among the lowest in France. APL of 4.91 — 4th best. Alsace's Protestant heritage and proximity to the Strasbourg teaching hospital give it excellent medical infrastructure and a relatively dry bar culture. The two infrastructure networks are genuinely independent.

Cher: a department that kept its bars and lost its doctors

The Cher is a useful lens. At 8.5 bars per 10,000, it sits above the national average of 7.3. But at APL 2.25 — the worst population-weighted average of any mainland département — it is also a place where 60% of residents cannot get reasonable access to a GP. The village café and the village surgery tracked the same rural decline, but at different speeds. The café, cheaper to run and embedded in social life, often outlasted the medical practice by years.

Now compare two Paris suburbs. Paris itself (75): 14.6 bars per 10,000, APL of 4.96, zero desert communes. The capital wins on both. Val-d'Oise (95): 3.7 bars per 10,000, APL of 2.41, 63% of residents in a medical desert. The suburb loses on both. The outer ring of Paris is simultaneously France's least bar-dense territory and one of its worst medical deserts. A different kind of emptying — not the slow rural retreat of the countryside, but a suburban gap that grew as fast as the population did.

A note on the data. Bar locations come from the SIRENE business registry (active establishments, NAF code 56.30Z, March 2026 snapshot) — the same dataset used in Where France drinks. Medical access scores are DREES APL 2023, the same data underpinning Where the doctors aren't. The join uses the INSEE commune code present in both datasets. Commune population comes from APL data (INSEE 2021 base). The 2x2 analysis covers communes with population ≥ 200 to exclude near-uninhabited hamlets.
