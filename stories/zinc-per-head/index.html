Zinc per head

Divide France's 49,385 bars by population and the ranking inverts: Corsican villages and alpine ski towns float to the top, while the Paris suburbs barely register.

2026-05-11 4 min read

The previous story mapped 49,385 bars as amber dots — France at a glance. But raw counts favour big places. Paris has 3,120 bars; Lozère has 135. Of course Paris wins. The more interesting question: which France drinks most per person?

Divide each département's bar count by its resident population, and a different country appears. The national average is 7.5 bars per 10,000 residents. Below is every metropolitan département, ranked.

The island that drinks, and the highlands that held on

Haute-Corse leads with 21.1 bars per 10,000 — nearly three times the national average. Corse-du-Sud is third at 16.9. The island's bar culture is dense: small villages where the café is still the social centre, tourist flows that sustain licences year-round, and a tradition of the épicerie-bar that blurs the category entirely.

Between the two Corsican departments sits Lozère — the least-populated département in metropolitan France, at 17.6/10k. Cantal follows (16.6), then Hautes-Alpes (15.6). These are the rural highlands: thin population, long memories, and a café that the next village over lost ten years ago. Lozère's 135 bars serve 76,519 people. They are not going anywhere.

Paris ranks 7th, and that's not a surprise

Paris (75) sits at 14.6/10k — seventh in the national ranking, well above average. The capital's 3,120 bars serve 2.1 million residents. The cliché of Paris as a café city holds up numerically. What surprises is the gap to its neighbours: Seine-et-Marne (3.8/10k), Val-d'Oise (3.7/10k), Yvelines (3.3/10k), Hauts-de-Seine (3.3/10k), Essonne (3.1/10k). The wealthiest, most densely planned ring around the capital has the fewest bars, per head, in metropolitan France.

The suburbs that forgot to build a café

The bottom six métro départements are all Île-de-France: Essonne, Yvelines, Hauts-de-Seine, Seine-et-Marne, Val-d'Oise, Val-de-Marne. These are the grands ensembles and pavillonnaire towns of the 1960s–80s. Urban plans included schools, RER stations, and car parks. The café was rarely in the programme. A town like Tournefeuille (29,439 residents, Haute-Garonne) has one bar in SIRENE: 0.3 per 10,000. Élancourt (Yvelines, 26,082 residents) has one. Some communes of 10,000–20,000 have none at all.

The ski resort effect — and the commune below it

At commune level, the ranking goes further. Alpine ski resorts dominate the top — Les Deux Alpes (Isère) clocks 201.8 bars per 10,000, Morzine (Haute-Savoie) 191.7. Their resident populations are counted in the thousands; their bar counts are calibrated for tens of thousands of winter visitors. These are not really outliers — they are a different use case entirely.

Below the resorts, the picture is more grounded. Rogliano (Haute-Corse, 540 residents, 7 bars) at 129.6. Deauville (Calvados, 3,565 residents, 32 bars) at 89.8. Saint-Tropez (Var, 3,578 residents, 34 bars) at 95.0. Tourism-driven coastal towns where the bar licence is a year-round economic anchor, even in January.

200,000 to 49,385

France had roughly 200,000 cafés and bars at its peak in the 1960s. Today's SIRENE count is 49,385. The long decline is real — driven by supermarkets, car culture, changing habits, and the slow attrition of the village café that couldn't find a buyer when the owner retired. But the decline is not uniform. Corse, Lozère, Cantal, the alpine valleys: places with strong local identity and seasonal tourism still carry dense networks. The zinc counter didn't vanish from France. It concentrated.

A note on the data. Bar counts come from INSEE SIRENE (NAF 56.30Z, active establishments, March 2026), with coordinates from the SIRENE Géolocalisation file — the same 49,385 points as the previous bars story. Population figures are from the DREES APL 2023 dataset (INSEE 2021 census base), used here for its commune-level population coverage across all 35,000 French communes. The join is on the 5-digit INSEE commune code. Overseas territories (97x) are excluded from département rankings. Commune-level rankings use a minimum population of 500 to limit small-denominator distortions.
